## 1. Where we are (concise)

- **The king called "O King" — 1.02 was a vocative, and it is a nominative (2026-11-04, branch `arena/79ff6faa-bhagavad-gita`).** The owner read the Nepali paraphrase and heard Duryodhana calling Droṇa "हे राजा". He was right. `ācāryam upasaṅgamya rājā vacanam abravīt` has `duryodhanaḥ` as its subject and `rājā` in apposition: "the king spoke these words". The edition proves the distinction itself — 1.16 renders nominative `rājā` as "King Yudhiṣṭhira", and the true vocative `rājan` is "O king" in 11.09 / 18.76 / 18.77 — so 1.02 was the single slip, and wrong twice: not a vocative, and pointing at the wrong listener (his first words are 1.03, addressing Droṇa as `ācārya`). Fixed in en/ne/hi, literal and paraphrase. **Same batch:** all 169 theme titles read against their spans; five rewording problems fixed — 1.10–1.11 (title named only 1.11 while its own description compared both armies), 8.16–8.19, 17.23–17.27, and the single-verse 17.07 / 17.28, which near-verbatim copied their part titles. 2.19–2.21 stays allowlisted. - **All 700 titles were read against their verses, and the printed line got the check it was missing (2026-11-04, branch `arena/79ff6faa-bhagavad-gita`).** The owner asked for a full read-through before publishing more of an ancient text under his name. The mutation pass found the real defect: appending a syllable to a pāda's printed Devanagari `d` **failed no check at all**, because every check reads `t` (build_gita derives `n` from `t`, site-health pins the method page to `n`, word rows are compared word by word). `check_padas.py` now transliterates each `d` and requires it to equal its `t` — **2,800 lines, 0 flags** — and counts akṣaras from `d` to pin the method page's 644 / 51 / five named irregulars at the reader-visible end. Proven by three mutations: extra syllable, dropped visarga (no syllable cost), and `ś → ṣ`. Counts unchanged: 68 Python, 943 assertions, 560 health, 10 SEO; shell untouched at 373,731.
- **One word, three glosses — on the rows, where the real split is (2026-11-04, branch `arena/79ff6faa-bhagavad-gita`).** D2 shipped in a different place than the brief asked, for a reason worth keeping: the printed verse is **sandhi-joined**. 2.47 prints `कर्मण्येवाधिकारस्ते` as one token while its word rows carry `कर्मणि / एव / अधिकारः / ते`, so no touched token can be mapped to a single word without inventing a boundary the text does not print. The peek therefore hangs off the **word rows**, which already carry the true split, and reveals the two languages the reader is not currently reading. Not gesture-only: tap, `Enter`/`Space` on the focused row, desktop right-click/menu key and `Escape`-to-close all work, and `aria-expanded` tracks it. Long press is 450 ms and any move past 10 px cancels, so a scroll flick never opens it. Each row carries its own `data-words` JSON because a verse has several containers (four pādas plus speaker lines) and a global would be overwritten by whichever rendered last. Checked against the data: all **9,484** word entries carry all three glosses, so a peek line can never be blank. 3 assertions added (940 → **943**). **Not verified here:** the touch gesture itself — no browser in this sandbox.
