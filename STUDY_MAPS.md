## Chapter 1 — 10 turns

The previously reviewed pilot map and teaching copy are retained.

1. **1.1–1.3 · The Blind King Asks** — Sañjaya answers with Duryodhana inspecting the Pāṇḍava ranks.
2. **1.4–1.9 · Duryodhana Sizes Up Both Armies** — He first names the Pāṇḍava champions, then his own commanders and fighters.
3. **1.10–1.11 · Guard Bhīṣma Above All** — Duryodhana compares the forces, then orders every division to hold position and protect Bhīṣma.
4. **1.12–1.13 · The Kaurava War Cry** — Bhīṣma’s roar and conch hearten Duryodhana; the Kaurava instruments sound together.
5. **1.14–1.19 · The Pāṇḍava Blast Shakes the Kauravas** — Kṛṣṇa and Arjuna lead the reply; Bhīma and the other Pāṇḍava heroes sound their conches.
6. **1.20–1.25 · Arjuna Asks to See the Armies** — Kṛṣṇa answers by placing the chariot before the assembled Kurus and inviting Arjuna to look.
7. **1.26–1.30 · Arjuna Sees His Kin—and Falters** — Compassion turns physical: Arjuna’s limbs fail, his mouth dries, and his mind reels.
8. **1.31–1.37 · No Victory Is Worth Killing His Kin** — Ill omens lead him to reject victory and kingdom; he argues that killing kin brings sin, not joy—not even dominion over the three worlds is worth it.
9. **1.38–1.44 · Arjuna Fears the Collapse of Family Dharma** — He links family destruction to adharma and varṇa-saṅkara, the loss of family and social duties, and ancestors deprived of their offerings.
10. **1.45–1.47 · He Would Rather Die—and Lays Down His Bow** — He calls the choice a great sin and would rather be killed unarmed; his final words give way to grief.

## Chapter 2 — 19 turns

The previously reviewed pilot map and teaching copy are retained.

1. **2.1–2.3 · Kṛṣṇa challenges Arjuna to rise** — Seeing Arjuna overwhelmed by grief, Kṛṣṇa challenges his despair and urges him to cast off weakness and rise.
2. **2.4–2.6 · Arjuna cannot strike his teachers** — Arjuna cannot bring himself to raise his bow against revered teachers; he would rather beg and cannot see whether victory or defeat is better.
3. **2.7–2.10 · Arjuna surrenders; Kṛṣṇa begins to teach** — Arjuna admits his confusion about duty and asks for guidance as Kṛṣṇa's disciple. No kingdom can ease his grief; when he falls silent, Kṛṣṇa begins to speak.
4. **2.11–2.15 · The Self endures as the body changes** — The wise do not grieve for the living or dead. The embodied Self passes through life's stages, while sense-contacts bring fleeting pleasure and pain to be endured.
5. **2.16–2.18 · The real does not perish; bodies do** — The unreal has no lasting being; the real does not cease. Bodies perish, but the imperishable Self that pervades them does not.
6. **2.19–2.21 · The Self neither kills nor is killed** — The Self is unborn, eternal and inexhaustible. Knowing this, one cannot regard it as the killer or the slain.
7. **2.22–2.25 · The Self sheds bodies as worn clothes** — As a person changes worn clothes for new ones, the embodied Self leaves old bodies and takes new ones. No weapon, fire, water or wind can alter it; it is unmanifest and unchanging.
8. **2.26–2.30 · Birth and death are certain; grieve for none** — Even if you think the Self is born and dies, birth and death are certain. Beings appear between unmanifest beginnings and ends; the embodied Self remains inviolable, so grieve for none.
9. **2.31–2.34 · Duty, sin, and the cost of turning away** — For a kṣatriya, a righteous war is a duty and an open door to heaven. To refuse it is to abandon one's dharma and glory, inviting sin and disgrace.
10. **2.35–2.38 · Honor, victory, and equanimity** — Other warriors may call Arjuna a coward. Slain, he gains heaven; victorious, he enjoys the earth. The instruction is to hold pleasure and pain, gain and loss, victory and defeat alike.
11. **2.39–2.41 · Yoga's wisdom is one-pointed** — The teaching turns from Sāṅkhya to yoga: no sincere effort is lost, and the resolute intellect holds to one aim rather than branching without end.
12. **2.42–2.46 · Beyond the Veda's promised rewards** — Those drawn to ritual rewards mistake them for the whole of the Veda. Rise beyond the three guṇas; to one who knows, the Vedas are like a well when water is everywhere.
13. **2.47–2.50 · Act without clinging to the fruits** — Act without making results your motive, and do not cling to inaction. Evenness in success and failure is yoga; yoga is skill in action.
14. **2.51–2.53 · Wisdom steadies the mind** — The wise let go of action's fruits and the bonds of birth. When the intellect crosses delusion and rests unmoved in samādhi, yoga is attained.
15. **2.54–2.58 · Arjuna asks how the steady sage lives** — Arjuna asks how the steady sage speaks, sits and moves. Kṛṣṇa begins the answer: content in the Self, untroubled by sorrow or joy, with the senses drawn in like a tortoise's limbs.
16. **2.59–2.61 · The taste remains until the Supreme is seen** — Objects may fall away while their taste remains; only seeing the Supreme removes that lingering relish. Until then, even the striving sage must restrain the turbulent senses.
17. **2.62–2.63 · How desire leads to ruin** — Dwelling on sense objects breeds attachment, desire and anger; anger clouds memory and judgment, and the person is lost.
18. **2.64–2.68 · Self-control brings serenity** — Moving among sense objects without attraction or aversion, the self-controlled attain serenity. Without peace there is no happiness, and the senses must be mastered.
19. **2.69–2.72 · The sage reaches the Brāhmī state** — What is night to all beings is waking to the sage; what the world sees as day is night to the seer. As rivers enter the unmoved ocean, desires enter the sage without disturbing him; this is the Brāhmī state.

## Chapter 3 — 10 turns

Combine the opening doubt with the need for action; distinguish reciprocity from the sustaining wheel; connect freedom to exemplary action before moving to agency, duty and desire.

1. **3.1–3.5 · Why action cannot be avoided** — Arjuna asks why he must act if knowledge is higher. Kṛṣṇa explains the two disciplines and why no one can remain actionless.
2. **3.6–3.9 · Act without hypocrisy or attachment** — Outward restraint with inward craving is hypocrisy. Master the senses, perform your duty, and act for sacrifice rather than attachment.
3. **3.10–3.13 · Sacrifice nourishes both gods and people** — Prajāpati establishes reciprocal nourishment through sacrifice. Receive with gratitude and share, rather than consuming only for yourself.
4. **3.14–3.16 · The wheel that sustains life** — Beings depend on food, rain, sacrifice and action, grounded in Brahman. To ignore this sustaining wheel and live for the senses is to live in vain.
5. **3.17–3.21 · The free person still acts for the world** — The Self-contented have nothing to gain, yet unattached action serves the world. Janaka and other leaders show how an example guides everyone.
6. **3.22–3.26 · Lead by acting, not by unsettling others** — Kṛṣṇa acts although he needs nothing, lest the world fall apart. The wise likewise support others through example without disturbing their understanding.
7. **3.27–3.30 · The guṇas act; surrender the work to me** — Ego claims to be the doer; the knower sees the guṇas at work. Do not unsettle partial knowers; surrender action to Kṛṣṇa and fight without fever.
8. **3.31–3.35 · Follow the teaching and your own dharma** — Faithful practice frees; fault-finding loses the way. Nature shapes action, but attraction and aversion must not rule you: follow your own dharma.
9. **3.36–3.39 · Desire and anger conceal wisdom** — Arjuna asks what drives wrongdoing. Kṛṣṇa names desire and anger, born of rajas: the all-devouring enemy that veils wisdom like smoke, dust or a membrane.
10. **3.40–3.43 · Master the senses and overcome desire** — Desire occupies senses, mind and intellect. Restrain the senses first; knowing the Self beyond intellect, steady yourself and overcome this enemy.

## Chapter 4 — 10 turns

Keep the birth question and reply together, then divine manifestation; follow action/inaction into the sage, sacrifices, the teacher, purifying knowledge and the final call to cut doubt.

1. **4.1–4.5 · Ancient yoga and the memory of past births** — The ancient yoga passed through Vivasvān and the royal sages, then was lost. Arjuna questions Kṛṣṇa’s age; Kṛṣṇa recalls their many past births.
2. **4.6–4.10 · Why the unborn Lord takes birth** — The unborn Lord appears through his own māyā to restore dharma. Knowing his divine birth and action, and taking refuge in him, leads beyond rebirth.
3. **4.11–4.14 · The Lord responds, yet action does not bind him** — People approach Kṛṣṇa through differing aims and worship. He establishes the fourfold order, yet remains the imperishable non-doer, untainted by action.
4. **4.15–4.18 · Discern action within inaction** — Act as the ancient seekers did, but understand action, forbidden action and inaction. The wise discern inaction in action, and action in inaction.
5. **4.19–4.23 · Unattached action leaves no bondage** — Free of selfish intention and attachment to results, the wise remain content and even-minded. Their action, offered as sacrifice, dissolves without bondage.
6. **4.24–4.28 · Sacrifice takes many forms** — From the vision of Brahman in every offering, sacrifice widens to restraint, sense activity, wealth, austerity, yoga and study.
7. **4.29–4.32 · Sacrifice purifies and leads to freedom** — Breath and regulated food too become sacrifice. Its remnants lead to Brahman; understand all these sacrifices as born of action and become free.
8. **4.33–4.36 · A teacher’s knowledge carries one across sin** — Knowledge-sacrifice is higher than material offering. Approach a teacher with inquiry and service; knowledge dispels delusion and carries even the sinner across.
9. **4.37–4.39 · Knowledge burns action; faith finds peace** — Knowledge burns action like fire burns fuel and is the supreme purifier. Faith, dedication and mastered senses bring knowledge and then supreme peace.
10. **4.40–4.42 · Cut doubt and stand firm in yoga** — Doubt destroys the path and its happiness. Renunciation through yoga and the sword of knowledge cut bondage: Kṛṣṇa calls Arjuna to rise.

## Chapter 5 — 6 turns

Bring the two paths into one opening; group the lotus image with non-doership, purification with the nine-gated city, knowledge with equal vision, and inward joy with the path to peace.

1. **5.1–5.5 · Renunciation and action share one goal** — Arjuna asks which way is better. Both lead to the highest good; karma-yoga excels, and the wise see Sāṅkhya and yoga as reaching the same goal.
2. **5.6–5.10 · Act without being stained: the lotus leaf** — Yoga prepares renunciation. The self-mastered knower sees the senses acting, offers work to Brahman and remains unstained like a lotus leaf.
3. **5.11–5.13 · Purifying work and the nine-gated city** — The yogi acts for inner purification and gives up the fruit for peace. Mentally renouncing action, the embodied one rests in the nine-gated city.
4. **5.14–5.18 · Knowledge removes ignorance and sees equally** — Nature acts; ignorance conceals knowledge. Self-knowledge shines like the sun, and the wise see equally across differences of birth and form.
5. **5.19–5.23 · Evenness and freedom from fleeting pleasures** — Equal vision steadies joy and disappointment. Happiness within outlasts sense pleasures; master desire and anger while still alive.
6. **5.24–5.29 · Inner joy culminates in liberation and peace** — The inwardly joyful sage, free of desire and anger, reaches Brahman-nirvāṇa. Sense and breath discipline, and knowing Kṛṣṇa as everyone’s friend, bring peace.

## Chapter 6 — 11 turns

Distinguish preparation from meditative experience, and peaceful practice from equal vision. Separate Arjuna’s failure question from Kṛṣṇa’s reassurance, retaining recovered insight with former practice.

1. **6.1–6.4 · Renunciation prepares the ascent in yoga** — Renunciation means relinquishing fruits and selfish intention, not merely rites. Action prepares the ascent; stillness belongs to the one established in yoga.
2. **6.5–6.9 · Self-mastery brings an equal vision** — Raise rather than degrade yourself: the self can be friend or enemy. Mastery brings steadiness in opposites and equal regard for things and people.
3. **6.10–6.15 · Meditation: solitude, posture and focus** — Prepare a quiet seat, hold the body upright and still the mind. With fearlessness, restraint and attention on Kṛṣṇa, meditation leads to peace.
4. **6.16–6.19 · Moderation steadies the windless lamp** — Neither excess nor deprivation supports yoga. Balanced food, effort and sleep let the mind rest in the Self, steady as a lamp without wind.
5. **6.20–6.23 · Stillness reveals joy beyond sorrow** — The stilled mind beholds the Self and finds joy beyond the senses. No gain is greater; even deep sorrow cannot shake it. Practise without discouragement.
6. **6.24–6.28 · Bring the wandering mind back to peace** — Let go of selfish desires and return the mind gently, again and again, to the Self. Persistent practice quiets passion and brings the joy of Brahman.
7. **6.29–6.32 · See the Self in everyone’s joy and pain** — Seeing the Self in all and all in Kṛṣṇa, the yogi lives in unity. Regard another’s pleasure and pain as your own: this is the highest yoga.
8. **6.33–6.36 · The restless mind yields to practice** — Arjuna says the mind is as hard to hold as the wind. Kṛṣṇa acknowledges its restlessness, but answers with practice, dispassion and proper effort.
9. **6.37–6.39 · Arjuna fears the fate of a fallen yogi** — What happens to the faithful seeker who fails? Arjuna fears being lost like a torn cloud and asks Kṛṣṇa to remove this doubt completely.
10. **6.40–6.44 · No good effort is lost across births** — Kṛṣṇa assures him that good effort never ends in ruin. A favourable rebirth restores earlier insight; former practice carries the seeker onward.
11. **6.45–6.47 · Perseverance ripens into wholehearted devotion** — Effort over many births brings perfection. Be a yogi; among yogis Kṛṣṇa esteems highest the one who worships him with faith and an inwardly devoted heart.

## Chapter 7 — 8 turns

Retain the already coherent opening turns. Split the final seven-verse passage at the real contrast: delusion in 24–27, freedom and knowledge at death in 28–30.

1. **7.1–7.3 · Hear how to know me fully** — With mind and refuge in Kṛṣṇa, hear knowledge together with realization. Few strive for perfection; rarer still is knowing him in truth.
2. **7.4–7.7 · Two natures, one thread through everything** — The lower nature has eight aspects; the higher sustains life. Both give rise to beings, and everything rests on Kṛṣṇa like pearls on one thread.
3. **7.8–7.11 · Recognise me in the world’s living essence** — Taste, light, fragrance, life, wisdom and strength reveal Kṛṣṇa’s presence. Even desire, when not opposed to dharma, belongs to this account.
4. **7.12–7.15 · Cross the guṇas’ delusion through refuge** — The guṇas arise from Kṛṣṇa yet conceal him from the deluded. His māyā is hard to cross; refuge in him opens the way beyond it.
5. **7.16–7.19 · Four devotees; the wise know Vāsudeva as all** — Distress, inquiry, need and wisdom bring people to devotion. All are noble; the wise, after many births, know Vāsudeva as everything.
6. **7.20–7.23 · Limited desires bring limited rewards** — Desire draws people to other deities. Kṛṣṇa steadies their faith and grants the fruit, but that fruit ends; his own devotees come to him.
7. **7.24–7.27 · Delusion hides the unborn Lord** — The unwise mistake the Unmanifest for a limited manifestation. Yoga-māyā and the opposites of desire and aversion veil the unborn, imperishable Lord.
8. **7.28–7.30 · Beyond delusion, know me even at death** — Freed from duality, the steadfast worship Kṛṣṇa. Seeking release from ageing and death, they know Brahman, all of adhyātma and action, and know him even at death.

## Chapter 8 — 7 turns

Join questions to definitions; keep ongoing remembrance with the final thought. Move verse 16 into the cosmic-return unit, rather than leaving it with the no-return practice.

1. **8.1–8.4 · Arjuna’s questions and Kṛṣṇa’s definitions** — Arjuna asks about Brahman, adhyātma, action, the elements, deities and sacrifice. Kṛṣṇa defines them before explaining remembrance at death.
2. **8.5–8.10 · Lifelong remembrance shapes the final thought** — The final remembered state directs departure. Therefore remember Kṛṣṇa while acting; steady practice, devotion and yogic concentration prepare the last moment.
3. **8.11–8.15 · Om and devotion lead beyond rebirth** — For the imperishable goal, restrain the body’s gates and depart uttering Om. Constant, single-minded remembrance makes Kṛṣṇa easy to reach, without return.
4. **8.16–8.19 · Even Brahmā’s world returns in cosmic cycles** — Even Brahmā’s world is subject to return. Through his vast days and nights, beings repeatedly emerge from the unmanifest and dissolve into it.
5. **8.20–8.22 · The imperishable abode beyond the cycles** — Beyond the dissolving unmanifest is the eternal, imperishable goal. It is Kṛṣṇa’s supreme abode, reached through undivided devotion, with no return.
6. **8.23–8.26 · The bright path and the returning path** — Kṛṣṇa contrasts the bright northern path to Brahman with the dark southern path that returns. The teaching names the two paths and their different outcomes.
7. **8.27–8.28 · Remain in yoga beyond ritual rewards** — Knowing the two paths prevents delusion: remain in yoga at all times. Such knowledge carries the yogi beyond the promised fruits of rites to the primal abode.

## Chapter 9 — 8 turns

Follow cosmic support, misunderstanding, true worship, the Lord’s universality, finite versus devoted destinations, offering, and the inclusive final promise.

1. **9.1–9.3 · The royal secret asks for faith** — Knowledge with realization is the royal secret, purifying and imperishable. Faith opens this path; without it, one returns to death and rebirth.
2. **9.4–9.8 · The Lord sustains and renews the world** — All beings rest in Kṛṣṇa, who remains beyond containment. Through prakṛti they dissolve and appear again, as the cycles of creation unfold.
3. **9.9–9.12 · Unbound creator, misunderstood in human form** — Creation does not bind its unattached Lord. Yet the deluded dismiss his human appearance; their hopes, actions and knowledge are lost to delusion.
4. **9.13–9.15 · Great souls worship in many ways** — Great souls know the imperishable source and worship steadfastly. Praise, vows, bowing and knowledge-sacrifice express devotion to the one and the manifold.
5. **9.16–9.19 · I am the offering, its goal and life itself** — Kṛṣṇa identifies himself with sacrifice, parents, refuge, knowledge, rain and heat—even life and death. Worship meets the source and sustainer of everything.
6. **9.20–9.25 · Temporary rewards—or coming to me** — Heaven’s pleasures end when merit is spent. Kṛṣṇa supplies what devotees lack and protects what they have; worship shapes the destination, and his devotees come to him.
7. **9.26–9.29 · Offer everything with devotion** — A simple leaf or every act can be offered with devotion. Dedication frees action from bondage; the impartial Lord dwells with those who worship him.
8. **9.30–9.34 · Devotion transforms and opens the way** — Past wrongdoing or birth does not bar devoted refuge. Kṛṣṇa closes with the same four instructions found in 18.65: fix your mind, be devoted, worship and bow.

## Chapter 10 — 11 turns

Keep recognition separate from the request for meditation aids. Break the long catalogue at a change of subject: beings/powers, then time/language/life/death. Join the seed to the concluding summary.

1. **10.1–10.3 · Know the unborn source of gods and sages** — Kṛṣṇa speaks again for Arjuna’s good. Gods and sages do not know his origin; knowing him as unborn and beginningless frees one from sin.
2. **10.4–10.7 · All qualities and ancient lineages arise from me** — The varied qualities of beings, great seers and ancient Manus arise from Kṛṣṇa. Knowing this glory brings unwavering yoga.
3. **10.8–10.11 · Devotion is answered with understanding** — Knowing Kṛṣṇa as the source, devotees rejoice together in him. He gives discerning yoga and lights the lamp that dispels ignorance.
4. **10.12–10.15 · Arjuna recognises the supreme Lord** — Arjuna affirms what the sages and Kṛṣṇa declare: the supreme Brahman and eternal Person. None knows this manifestation fully; the Lord alone knows himself.
5. **10.16–10.18 · Tell me how to recognise and remember you** — Arjuna asks for the glories that pervade the world and the forms in which to contemplate Kṛṣṇa. He is never satisfied with hearing this nectar.
6. **10.19–10.23 · Glories in the Self, gods and cosmic lights** — The endless list begins in outline: the Self in every heart, the sun and moon, Vedas, mind, consciousness, deities, fire and mountain.
7. **10.24–10.26 · Glories in sacred leaders, Om and nature** — Priest, commander, ocean, seer, Om, japa, Himālaya and tree become signs of Kṛṣṇa’s presence, alongside celestial sages and the perfected.
8. **10.27–10.31 · Glories among living beings and great powers** — Horse, elephant, king, weapon, serpent, lion, bird, wind, warrior and river illustrate the Lord’s excellence throughout the living world.
9. **10.32–10.34 · Glories in time, language, life and death** — The list turns to beginnings and ends, Self-knowledge, debate, letters, timeless ordering, death and birth, and the powers of speech, memory and wisdom.
10. **10.35–10.38 · Glories in song, seasons and human excellence** — Hymn, metre, month and spring lead to brilliance, resolve, exemplary people, wise policy and silence—the glories woven through human life.
11. **10.39–10.42 · One source, endless glories, one sustaining part** — Every being has Kṛṣṇa as its seed; his glories have no end. All splendour is a spark of him, and one part of him sustains the whole world.

## Chapter 11 — 12 turns

Preserve changes of speaker and emotional turns: request, vision, awe, fear, destruction, Time’s reply, hymn, apology, request for the gentle form, restoration and devotion.

1. **11.1–11.4 · Arjuna asks to see the cosmic form** — Having heard the teaching, Arjuna asks to see the imperishable divine form—if Kṛṣṇa judges that he can see it.
2. **11.5–11.8 · The gift of the divine eye** — Kṛṣṇa invites Arjuna to behold countless forms and the whole world in one body. Ordinary sight is insufficient: he grants the divine eye.
3. **11.9–11.14 · Sañjaya narrates the overwhelming vision** — Sañjaya describes faces, weapons and radiance like a thousand suns. Arjuna sees the universe gathered into one and bows in wonder.
4. **11.15–11.20 · Arjuna beholds an endless, blazing universe** — Arjuna sees gods and beings within the immeasurable form, without beginning or end. Sun-and-moon eyes and fiery mouths fill the worlds with awe.
5. **11.21–11.25 · Wonder becomes terror; Arjuna seeks refuge** — Heavenly hosts gaze, enter and praise; the worlds and Arjuna tremble. The blazing mouths leave him without direction or peace, pleading for grace.
6. **11.26–11.30 · Warriors rush into the devouring mouths** — Warriors from both sides enter the terrible mouths, crushed between teeth. Rivers and moths portray their rush as the form devours the worlds.
7. **11.31–11.34 · Time’s decree and Arjuna’s task** — Arjuna asks who this terrible form is. Kṛṣṇa answers: Time, destroyer of worlds. The warriors are already slain; Arjuna must rise as his instrument.
8. **11.35–11.40 · Trembling gives way to a hymn of praise** — Arjuna bows with a faltering voice, then praises the primal Lord, greater than Brahmā, knower and known, embracing every direction.
9. **11.41–11.44 · Forgive the familiarity of friendship** — Remembering careless words and casual treatment, Arjuna asks forgiveness. Before the father and teacher of the world, he prostrates like a child or friend.
10. **11.45–11.46 · Show me the familiar, gracious form** — Joy and fear remain together. Arjuna asks for the familiar crowned, four-armed form with mace and discus, rather than the overwhelming universal form.
11. **11.47–11.50 · Grace restores the gentle form** — Kṛṣṇa explains the rare vision granted by grace, calms Arjuna’s fear and shows his own gracious form again.
12. **11.51–11.55 · Restored to calm, learn undivided devotion** — Composed again, Arjuna hears that undivided devotion alone knows, sees and enters this form. Work for Kṛṣṇa, unattached and without enmity, leads to him.

## Chapter 12 — 5 turns

Bring the manifest/unmanifest comparison together; join deliverance with the instruction to focus the heart. Keep the practice ladder intact and give the devotee’s qualities two memorable turns.

1. **12.1–12.5 · Devotion or the unmanifest: the choice of path** — Arjuna compares devotion with the unmanifest path. Both reach Kṛṣṇa, but faith-filled personal devotion is praised; the unmanifest is harder for the embodied.
2. **12.6–12.8 · Surrender and fix the heart on me** — Kṛṣṇa swiftly delivers those who dedicate action and meditate on him. Fix mind and intellect in him: the promise is to dwell in him.
3. **12.9–12.12 · A ladder of practice ending in peace** — A graded path: if concentration is difficult, practise; if that is difficult, work for Kṛṣṇa. If needed, relinquish action’s fruits: renunciation brings peace.
4. **12.13–12.16 · Compassion and self-mastery make a dear devotee** — The dear devotee hates no being, is compassionate and steady, neither disturbs the world nor is disturbed by it, and acts without anxious claims.
5. **12.17–12.20 · Evenness and faithful devotion complete the path** — Equal through praise and blame, friend and foe, the devotee remains content and steady. Those who follow this immortal dharma with faith are exceedingly dear.

## Chapter 13 — 6 turns

Replace fourteen fragments with six teaching turns: field, preparation for knowledge, knowable Brahman, nature/experiencer, paths to equal vision, and the untouched Self. Keep the complete Brahman portrait together.

1. **13.1–13.6 · The field, its knower and its contents** — The body is the field; Kṛṣṇa is its knower in every field. The teaching names its sources, elements, senses, desires and changing experiences.
2. **13.7–13.11 · The dispositions that make knowledge possible** — Humility, non-violence, restraint, detachment, devotion and sustained Self-knowledge prepare understanding. These dispositions are themselves declared knowledge.
3. **13.12–13.18 · Brahman: the knowable light in every heart** — Beginningless Brahman pervades all, beyond senses and divisions, sustaining every being. It is the light of lights in every heart; knowing this leads to Kṛṣṇa’s being.
4. **13.19–13.23 · Nature, the experiencer and release from birth** — Prakṛti acts and puruṣa experiences; attachment to the guṇas brings birth. Know the supreme witness in the body and the truth of nature to pass beyond rebirth.
5. **13.24–13.28 · Different paths, one Lord in all beings** — Meditation, knowledge, action and faithful hearing lead to vision. Seeing the imperishable Lord equally in every being, one does not harm the Self and reaches the goal.
6. **13.29–13.34 · The Self does not act and remains unstained** — Nature acts; the Self remains untouched like space and illumines the field like the sun. Seeing the one within the many and distinguishing field from knower brings freedom.

## Chapter 14 — 7 turns

Group the binding mechanisms, prevailing signs and consequences separately, rather than giving each two-verse observation its own theme. Preserve Arjuna’s question, the witness’s marks and devotion as distinct turns.

1. **14.1–14.4 · Liberating knowledge and the birth of beings** — The highest knowledge leads beyond creation and dissolution. Kṛṣṇa describes the great womb and himself as the seed-giving father of all forms.
2. **14.5–14.9 · Three guṇas, three ways of binding** — Sattva binds through happiness and knowledge, rajas through craving and action, tamas through ignorance and inertia. All three bind the embodied Self.
3. **14.10–14.13 · Recognise which guṇa is prevailing** — The guṇas overcome one another in turn. Illumination marks sattva; greed and restless activity mark rajas; darkness and confusion mark tamas.
4. **14.14–14.18 · The guṇas shape fruit, rebirth and destiny** — A prevailing guṇa shapes the next birth and the fruit of action. Knowledge, greed and delusion lead respectively upward, to the middle, or downward.
5. **14.19–14.21 · Seeing beyond the guṇas: Arjuna asks its marks** — Seeing the guṇas as the agents and knowing what is beyond them brings freedom from ageing and death. Arjuna asks how such a person is recognised and lives.
6. **14.22–14.25 · The steady witness amid every opposite** — Neither resisting nor craving the guṇas, the witness stays unmoved. Equal through pleasure, pain, praise, blame, friend and foe, the person stands beyond them.
7. **14.26–14.27 · Devotion crosses the guṇas to Brahman** — Unwavering devotion to Kṛṣṇa crosses the guṇas and makes one fit for Brahman. He is the basis of immortality, eternal dharma and absolute bliss.

## Chapter 15 — 5 turns

Keep cutting the tree with seeking its source; group the whole soul-journey and its recognition. Join light, nourishment, digestion and knowledge as divine sustenance; gather the three-person distinction and conclusion.

1. **15.1–15.4 · Cut the world-tree and seek its source** — The upside-down tree grows through guṇas and attachment to action. Cut its roots with detachment, then seek the primal Person and the state without return.
2. **15.5–15.6 · Who reaches the imperishable abode** — Free of pride, delusion, attachment and opposing pleasures, the undeluded reach the supreme abode. Neither sun, moon nor fire lights it; there is no return.
3. **15.7–15.11 · The soul’s journey and the eyes that see it** — An eternal portion of Kṛṣṇa carries mind and senses from body to body, as wind carries fragrance. The disciplined see this; the deluded fail to recognise it.
4. **15.12–15.15 · The Lord sustains world, body and understanding** — Light, nourishment, digestion, memory, knowledge and forgetfulness arise from Kṛṣṇa. Seated in every heart, he is the one to be known through the Vedas.
5. **15.16–15.20 · The Supreme Person beyond both kinds of being** — Beyond the perishable and imperishable stands the sustaining Supreme Person. Knowing Kṛṣṇa as Puruṣottama brings wholehearted worship and completes this secret teaching.

## Chapter 16 — 5 turns

Make the five turns explicit: contrasting natures, false worldview, craving/boast, empty worship and downfall, then the practical exit. Never split the continuous self-boast in 13–15.

1. **16.1–16.5 · Divine and demoniac: freedom or bondage** — The divine qualities are set against hypocrisy, pride and anger. One nature frees, the other binds; Kṛṣṇa reassures Arjuna that he is born to the divine.
2. **16.6–16.10 · A false view becomes destructive conduct** — The demoniac reject truth, purity, a foundation and a Lord. Insatiable desire and false conviction turn that worldview into harmful deeds.
3. **16.11–16.16 · Desire, boast and the net of delusion** — Boundless worry and unjust gain feed the boast of wealth, power and superiority. Entangled in desire and false self-praise, the deluded fall into misery.
4. **16.17–16.20 · Empty sacrifice and hatred deepen the fall** — Sacrifice becomes display without rule; ego turns to hatred of the Lord in oneself and others. Repeated demoniac births carry this conduct downward.
5. **16.21–16.24 · Close hell’s gates and let scripture guide action** — Abandon desire, anger and greed—the three gates of ruin. Do not follow impulse alone; scripture must guide what to do and what not to do.

## Chapter 17 — 11 turns

The previously reviewed pilot map and teaching copy are retained.

1. **17.1–17.3 · Faith takes three forms, shaped by nature** — Arjuna asks about those who worship with faith but without following scripture; Kṛṣṇa replies that faith is threefold and follows each person's nature.
2. **17.4 · Three kinds of worshippers** — Sāttvic people worship the gods; rājasic people worship yakṣas and rākṣasas; tāmasic people worship ghosts and spirits.
3. **17.5–17.6 · Those who torture themselves are demoniac** — Driven by hypocrisy, ego and desire, some perform unscriptural austerities that torment the body and the Lord dwelling within it; their resolve is demoniac.
4. **17.7 · Food, sacrifice, austerity and giving are each threefold** — Food, sacrifice, austerity and gifts each have three forms; hear how they differ.
5. **17.8–17.10 · The food each nature loves** — Food dear to each is threefold — sāttvic, rājasic and tāmasic; hear what each is.
6. **17.11–17.13 · Sacrifice by rule, for reward, or without faith** — Sāttvic sacrifice follows scriptural rule without desire for fruit; rājasic sacrifice seeks fruit or display; tāmasic sacrifice lacks rule, food offerings, mantra, fees and faith.
7. **17.14–17.16 · Austerity of the body, speech and mind** — Austerity is threefold: of the body, of speech, and of the mind.
8. **17.17–17.19 · Three kinds of austerity** — Austerity performed with supreme faith and without desire for fruit is sāttvic; austerity for honour and display is rājasic; austerity involving self-torture is tāmasic.
9. **17.20–17.22 · Three kinds of giving** — Sāttvic giving is made to a worthy recipient at the right time and place without expectation; rājasic giving seeks a return or is reluctant; tāmasic giving is disrespectful and contemptuous.
10. **17.23–17.27 · Om Tat Sat: the threefold designation of Brahman** — Om, Tat and Sat are the threefold designation of Brahman. Om marks the start of prescribed sacrifice, giving and austerity; acts done with Tat are performed without desire for fruit; Sat denotes reality, goodness and steadfastness.
11. **17.28 · Acts without faith are called Asat** — Anything offered, given, performed or done without faith is called Asat; it has no value here or hereafter.

## Chapter 18 — 18 turns

Gather the opening definition and retained duties; complete the five-causes discussion before the knowledge classification. Pair action with doer, separate preparation for Brahman from mature devotion, and keep reflection before the final secret.

1. **18.1–18.6 · Renunciation is not abandonment of purifying duties** — Arjuna asks about sannyāsa and tyāga. Kṛṣṇa distinguishes desired action from its fruits: sacrifice, giving and austerity must continue, without attachment.
2. **18.7–18.12 · Three kinds of relinquishment and their fruits** — Deluded abandonment is tāmasic, fear-driven abandonment rājasic; duty without attachment is sāttvic. True relinquishment gives up the fruit, not all bodily action.
3. **18.13–18.17 · Five causes, not the Self alone, produce action** — Body, agent, instruments, effort and destiny accomplish action. Mistaking the Self alone for the doer is ignorance; freedom from ego removes bondage.
4. **18.18–18.22 · How the guṇas shape knowledge** — After naming the impulse and basis of action, Kṛṣṇa classifies knowledge: seeing the one, seeing separate beings, or clinging to a fragment as the whole.
5. **18.23–18.28 · Action and doer under the three guṇas** — Compare action with its doer: duty and steadiness without ego, craving and agitation, or deluded action with heedlessness and inertia.
6. **18.29–18.32 · Three intellects distinguish—or distort—duty** — The intellect may clearly distinguish action, duty, fear and freedom; recognise them imperfectly; or invert right and wrong under darkness.
7. **18.33–18.35 · Three kinds of resolve: what do you hold?** — Resolve holds mind and senses steadily, clings to aims for their fruit, or refuses to release sleep, fear and sorrow. The guṇas shape what persists.
8. **18.36–18.39 · Happiness changes from poison to nectar—or back** — Sāttvic happiness matures from difficulty into clarity; rājasic pleasure reverses into pain. Tāmasic happiness deludes through sleep, sloth and heedlessness.
9. **18.40–18.44 · Nature gives different duties to different lives** — No being is free of the guṇas. The four varṇas’ duties express qualities born of nature: wisdom, courage, livelihood and service.
10. **18.45–18.48 · One’s own work becomes worship and perfection** — Worship the source of beings through your own duty. Natural work, even imperfect, need not be abandoned: every undertaking has faults like fire has smoke.
11. **18.49–18.53 · Renunciation prepares one for Brahman** — Freedom from attachment leads to freedom from action. Purified intellect, restraint, solitude, meditation and release of ego make one fit for Brahman.
12. **18.54–18.55 · Serenity opens to supreme devotion** — The Brahman-realised one is serene, equal to all, without grief or craving. Supreme devotion reveals Kṛṣṇa in truth and leads into him.
13. **18.56–18.60 · Dedicate action; ego cannot defeat nature** — Resting in Kṛṣṇa, dedicate every act and cross difficulties by grace. An ego-driven refusal to fight is vain: one’s own nature still compels action.
14. **18.61–18.63 · Refuge in the heart; reflect and choose** — The Lord dwells in every heart: take refuge wholly for peace. Kṛṣṇa then asks Arjuna to reflect fully on the teaching and act as he chooses.
15. **18.64–18.66 · The final promise: surrender and do not grieve** — Out of love, Kṛṣṇa gives the supreme secret: fix your mind on him, worship and bow. Take refuge in him alone; he promises freedom from all sin.
16. **18.67–18.71 · Who may receive, teach and hear this dialogue** — Do not give this teaching to one who lacks austerity or devotion, refuses to listen, or finds fault with Kṛṣṇa. Devoted teaching, study and faithful hearing are honoured; even the listener is freed.
17. **18.72–18.73 · Arjuna’s answer: my doubt is gone** — Kṛṣṇa asks whether the teaching was heard and delusion removed. Arjuna answers that memory is restored, doubt is gone, and he will act as instructed.
18. **18.74–18.78 · Sañjaya’s remembrance and final assurance** — Sañjaya rejoices in the dialogue and vision heard through Vyāsa’s grace. His closing assurance joins Kṛṣṇa and Arjuna with prosperity, victory and steadfast wisdom.
