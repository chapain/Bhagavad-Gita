18.1–18.6 Sannyāsa and tyāga: not abandonment of purifying duties
Study this theme in the app →

Arjuna asks about sannyāsa and tyāga. Kṛṣṇa distinguishes desired action from its fruits: sacrifice, giving and austerity must continue, without attachment.

18.1Arjuna asks how sannyāsa differs from tyāga
Arjuna opens the chapter by asking for the true meaning of sannyāsa and tyāga, and how the two differ.
18.2Sannyāsa drops desire-led acts; tyāga drops all fruits
Kṛṣṇa’s first answer: sannyāsa is setting aside desire-driven acts; tyāga is giving up the fruit of every act.
18.3The schools of thought
He sets out the debate: one school would drop all action as a fault, another insists that sacrifice, giving and austerity must stay.
18.4Tyāga is of three kinds
Kṛṣṇa promises his own firm verdict on tyāga and says it comes in three kinds.
18.5Sacrifice, gift and austerity are not to be given up
His verdict begins: sacrifice, giving and austerity are never to be dropped, because they purify even the wise.
18.6Perform these acts unattached; renounce their fruits
Even these duties are to be done without attachment and without claiming their fruit — this is Kṛṣṇa’s settled view.

niścayaṃ śṛṇu me tatra tyāge bharatasattama । tyāgo hi puruṣavyāghra trividhaḥ saṃprakīrtitaḥ

हे भरतश्रेष्ठ, त्याग के विषय में मेरा निश्चित निष्कर्ष सुन; हे नरश्रेष्ठ, त्याग तीन प्रकार का कहा गया है।

yajñadānatapaḥkarma na tyājyaṃ kāryameva tat । yajño dānaṃ tapaścaiva pāvanāni manīṣiṇām
The acts of sacrifice, gift and austerity are not to be given up; they must be performed, for they purify even the wise.“Sacrifice, charity, and penance should never be renounced — they purify even the wise and must be performed.”
यज्ञ, दान र तपस्या-कर्महरू त्याग्नु हुँदैन; ती गर्नैपर्छ, किनकि ती ज्ञानीलाई पनि शुद्ध पार्छन्।
यज्ञ, दान और तपस्या के कर्म नहीं त्यागने चाहिए; उन्हें अवश्य करना चाहिए, क्योंकि वे ज्ञानियों को भी शुद्ध करते हैं।

एतान्यपि तु कर्माणि सङ्गं त्यक्त्वा फलानि च । कर्तव्यानीति मे पार्थ निश्चितं मतमुत्तमम्
etānyapi tu karmāṇi saṅgaṃ tyaktvā phalāni ca । kartavyānīti me pārtha niścitaṃ matamuttamam
But even these actions should be performed, giving up attachment and the fruits, O Pārtha — this is my certain and best conviction.“Yet even these should be done without attachment and without longing for results — this is my firm and final view.”
तर हे पार्थ, यी कर्महरू पनि आसक्ति र फल त्यागेर गर्नुपर्छ — यो मेरो निश्चित र उत्तम विश्वास हो।
किन्तु हे पार्थ, ये कर्म भी आसक्ति और फल को त्यागकर करने चाहिए — यह मेरा निश्चित और उत्तम विश्वास है।

18.7–18.12 Three kinds of relinquishment and their fruits
Study this theme in the app →

Deluded abandonment is tāmasic, fear-driven abandonment rājasic; duty without attachment is sāttvic. True relinquishment gives up the fruit, not all bodily action.

18.7Giving up duty through delusion is tāmasic
First kind: giving up prescribed duty out of delusion is tāmasic.
18.8Giving it up from fear of pain is rājasic
Second kind: giving up duty only because it is painful or tiring is rājasic, and it earns nothing.
18.9Doing it because it ought to be done
Third kind: doing duty simply because it must be done, free of attachment and fruit, is sāttvic.
18.10Neither dislikes nor craves
The sāttvic renouncer, clear and steady, neither resents hard work nor clings to pleasant work.
18.11No one can give up all action
Total inaction is impossible for an embodied being; the true renouncer is the one who gives up the fruit.
18.12Three kinds of fruit
The three kinds of fruit — pleasant, painful and mixed — follow those who do not renounce, never the one who does.

niyatasya tu saṃnyāsaḥ karmaṇo nopapadyate । mohāttasya parityāgastāmasaḥ parikīrtitaḥ
The renunciation of prescribed action is not proper; abandoning it through delusion is declared tāmasic.“Renouncing one's prescribed duty is wrong — abandoning it out of confusion is tāmasic.”
विहित कर्मको त्याग उचित होइन; मोहले त्यसलाई त्याग्नु तामस भनिएको छ।
विहित कर्म का त्याग उचित नहीं है; मोह से उसे त्यागना तामस कहा गया है।

दुःखमित्येव यत्कर्म कायक्लेशभयात्त्यजेत् । स कृत्वा राजसं त्यागं नैव त्यागफलं लभेत्
duḥkhamityeva yatkarma kāyakleśabhayāttyajet । sa kṛtvā rājasaṃ tyāgaṃ naiva tyāgaphalaṃ labhet
He who gives up action because it brings pain, from fear of bodily suffering — performing the rājasic abandonment, he does not gain the fruit of renunciation.“One who drops a duty because it is hard or painful makes a rājasic renunciation and gains nothing from it.”
जसले दुःख आउँछ भनी, शारीरिक कष्टको डरले कर्म त्याग्छ — राजस त्याग गर्ने त्यो व्यक्तिले त्यागको फल पाउँदैन।

The sāttvic renouncer, endowed with sattva and firm understanding, with doubts cut away, neither hates unpleasant action nor clings to pleasant.“The sāttvic renouncer, wise and clear, neither dislikes unpleasant work nor is attached to pleasant work.”
सत्त्वयुक्त, दृढ समझ भएको, सन्देहरहित सात्त्विक त्यागीले न अप्रिय कर्मलाई घृणा गर्छ, न प्रिय कर्ममा टाँसिन्छ।
सत्त्व-युक्त, दृढ़ समझ वाला, सन्देह-रहित सात्त्विक त्यागी न अप्रिय कर्म से घृणा करता है, न प्रिय कर्म में आसक्त होता है।

na hi dehabhṛtā śakyaṃ tyaktuṃ karmāṇyaśeṣataḥ । yastu karmaphalatyāgī sa tyāgītyabhidhīyate
Indeed, the embodied one cannot give up all actions entirely; but he who gives up the fruit of action is called a true renouncer.“No embodied being can abandon action altogether; but one who renounces the fruits of action is a true renouncer.”
निश्चय नै देहधारीले सबै कर्म पूरै त्याग्न सक्दैन; तर जसले कर्मफल त्याग्छ, ऊ साँचो त्यागी भनिन्छ।
निश्चय ही देहधारी सब कर्मों का पूर्ण त्याग नहीं कर सकता; किन्तु जो कर्म-फल का त्याग करता है, वह सच्चा त्यागी कहलाता है।
18.12 ↗
अनिष्टमिष्टं मिश्रं च त्रिविधं कर्मणः फलम् । भवत्यत्यागिनां प्रेत्य न तु संन्यासिनां क्वचित्
aniṣṭamiṣṭaṃ miśraṃ ca trividhaṃ karmaṇaḥ phalam । bhavatyatyāgināṃ pretya na tu saṃnyāsināṃ kvacit
The threefold fruit of action — pleasant, unpleasant and mixed — comes after death to the non-renouncer; but never to the renouncer.“Cling to results and three kinds of fruit follow you past death — sweet, bitter and mixed. Let them go and none does.”
त्याग नगर्नेहरूका निम्ति कर्मको त्रिविध फल — प्रिय, अप्रिय र मिश्रित — मृत्युपछि आउँछ; त्यागीका निम्ति कहिल्यै होइन।
अ-त्यागी को कर्म का त्रिविध फल — प्रिय, अप्रिय और मिश्रित — मृत्यु के बाद मिलता है; त्यागी को कभी नहीं।

18.13–18.17 Five causes, not the Self alone, produce action
Study this theme in the app →

Body, agent, instruments, effort and destiny accomplish action. Mistaking the Self alone for the doer is ignorance; freedom from ego removes bondage.

18.13Learn the five causes
A new question: what brings action about? Kṛṣṇa begins to name the five causes behind every deed.
18.14Destiny, the fifth cause
The list: the body as the seat, the doer, the instruments, the many kinds of effort, and destiny as the fifth.
18.15Whatever action a person undertakes
Every deed of body, speech or mind, right or wrong, has these same five causes.
18.16Seeing the Self alone as the doer is mistaken
So whoever, with an unrefined mind, sees the Self alone as the doer does not see truly.
18.17Free of ego, he slays and is not bound
One free of the feeling “I am the doer” is not bound by what he does — even if he were to strike down these worlds.

18.18–18.22 How the guṇas shape knowledge
Study this theme in the app →

After naming the impulse and basis of action, Kṛṣṇa classifies knowledge: seeing the one, seeing separate beings, or clinging to a fragment as the whole.

18.18Knowledge, action, agent
The impulse to act is knowledge, the known and the knower; the basis of action is instrument, act and agent.
18.19Knowledge, action, agent: threefold by the guṇas
Knowledge, action and agent each divide into three by the guṇas, and Kṛṣṇa takes them in turn.
18.20The sāttvic knowledge
Sāttvic knowledge sees one undivided reality in all beings.
18.21Rājasic knowledge sees beings as separate
Rājasic knowledge sees only separate and varied beings.
18.22Irrational, clinging to a part
Tāmasic knowledge clings to one small thing as if it were everything, without reason or truth.

ज्ञानं ज्ञेयं परिज्ञाता त्रिविधा कर्मचोदना । करणं कर्म कर्तेति त्रिविधः कर्मसंग्रहः
jñānaṃ jñeyaṃ parijñātā trividhā karmacodanā । karaṇaṃ karma karteti trividhaḥ karmasaṃgrahaḥ

18.23–18.28 Action and doer under the three guṇas

Compare action with its doer: duty and steadiness without ego, craving and agitation, or deluded action with heedlessness and a lazy doer.

18.23Sāttvic action seeks no fruit
Sāttvic action: prescribed duty, done without attachment, liking or dislike, and with no eye on the fruit.
18.24Rājasic action serves desire
Rājasic action: driven by desire and ego, and done with great strain.
18.25Done in delusion
Tāmasic action: begun in delusion, ignoring consequences, harm and one’s own ability.
18.26The sāttvic agent
The sāttvic doer is free of attachment and ego, steady, eager, and unshaken by success or failure.
18.27The rājasic agent
The rājasic doer is passionate, greedy for results, and swung between joy and sorrow.
18.28Deceitful, lazy, stubborn
The tāmasic doer is undisciplined, deceitful, lazy, and always putting things off.

18.36–18.39 Happiness changes from poison to nectar—or back

Sāttvic happiness matures from difficulty into clarity; rājasic pleasure reverses into pain. Tāmasic happiness deludes through sleep, sloth and heedlessness.

18.36The threefold happiness
Kṛṣṇa now turns to three kinds of happiness, one of which ends sorrow.
18.37Poison at first, nectar at the end
Sāttvic happiness is bitter as poison at first and sweet as nectar in the end, born of a mind clear and at rest in the Self.
18.38Sweet at first, poison at last
Rājasic happiness is the reverse: sweet from the senses at first, poison in the end.
18.39Delusive from beginning to end
Tāmasic happiness is delusion from start to finish, rooted in sleep, laziness and heedlessness.

No being is free of the guṇas. The four varṇas’ duties express qualities born of nature: wisdom, courage, livelihood and service.

18.40No one free of the guṇas
Nothing on earth or in heaven is free of the three guṇas, so they shape human duties too.
18.41The fourfold duties
Hence the duties of the four varṇas are divided by the qualities born of each one’s nature.
18.42The brāhmaṇa's duty, born of his nature
The brāhmaṇa’s work, born of his nature: calm, self-control, austerity, purity, patience, honesty, knowledge and faith.
18.43The kṣatriya's duty, born of his nature
The kṣatriya’s: courage, brilliance, steadiness, skill, not fleeing battle, generosity and leadership.
18.44The vaiśya's and the śūdra's duty
The vaiśya’s: farming, herding and trade; the śūdra’s: service.

18.54–18.55 Serenity opens to supreme devotion

The Brahman-realised one is serene, equal to all, without grief or craving. Supreme devotion reveals Kṛṣṇa in truth and leads into him.

18.54The Brahman-realised one neither grieves nor desires
One who has become Brahman is serene, neither grieves nor craves, sees all beings alike, and gains supreme devotion to Kṛṣṇa.
18.55Devotion reveals me in truth
Through devotion he knows Kṛṣṇa as he truly is, and having known him, enters him at once.

18.56–18.60 Dedicate action; ego cannot defeat nature

Resting in Kṛṣṇa, dedicate every act and cross difficulties by grace. An ego-driven refusal to fight is vain: one’s own nature still compels action.

18.56By my grace, the eternal state
Even while doing every action, one who takes refuge in Kṛṣṇa reaches the eternal state by his grace.
18.57Surrender all actions to me
Arjuna is told to offer all actions to Kṛṣṇa in thought, hold him as the highest aim, and keep his mind fixed on him through discernment.
18.58If from ego you will not listen, you perish
With his mind on Kṛṣṇa, Arjuna will cross every difficulty by grace; if ego makes him refuse to listen, he will be lost.
18.59Your nature will drive you
His resolve “I will not fight” is empty: it comes from ego, and his own nature will compel him anyway.
18.60Your own nature will compel you
Bound by action born of his own nature, he will helplessly do what he now refuses to do.

Do not give this teaching to one who lacks austerity or devotion, refuses to listen, or finds fault with Kṛṣṇa. Devoted teaching, study and faithful hearing are honoured; even the listener is freed.

18.67Who should not receive this teaching
This teaching is not for one without austerity or devotion, who will not listen, or who speaks against Kṛṣṇa.
18.68The teacher's reward
Whoever shares it with Kṛṣṇa’s devotees, out of supreme devotion, will surely come to him.
18.69No one dearer
No one serves Kṛṣṇa better than such a teacher, and none will be dearer to him.
18.70The reward of studying
Even studying this dialogue is worship of Kṛṣṇa by the sacrifice of knowledge.
18.71The faithful listener is freed
Even one who only listens, with faith and without fault-finding, is freed and reaches the worlds of the righteous.

18.72–18.73 Arjuna’s answer: my doubt is gone

Kṛṣṇa asks whether the teaching was heard and delusion removed. Arjuna answers that memory is restored, doubt is gone, and he will act as instructed.

18.72The Lord's question
Kṛṣṇa turns to Arjuna: was this heard with a focused mind, and has the delusion of ignorance gone?
18.73My delusion is gone; I will do your word
Arjuna replies: delusion gone, memory regained, doubt dispelled — he will act on Kṛṣṇa’s word.

18.74–18.78 Sañjaya’s remembrance and final assurance
Study this theme in the app →

Sañjaya rejoices in the dialogue and vision heard through Vyāsa’s grace. His closing assurance joins Kṛṣṇa and Arjuna with prosperity, victory and steadfast wisdom.

18.74Thus I heard this wondrous dialogue
Closing the narrative, Sañjaya tells Dhṛtarāṣṭra he has heard this thrilling dialogue between Kṛṣṇa and Arjuna.
18.75The grace of Vyāsa
By Vyāsa’s grace he heard the supreme secret of yoga directly from Kṛṣṇa himself.
18.76The joy of remembering
He rejoices again and again as he recalls the holy conversation.
18.77Remembering that form, I rejoice again
And he is thrilled again and again by remembering Kṛṣṇa’s wondrous form.
18.78Where Kṛṣṇa and Arjuna are
The conclusion: where Kṛṣṇa and Arjuna are, there are fortune, victory, prosperity and steady righteousness.
