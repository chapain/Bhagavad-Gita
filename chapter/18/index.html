Arjuna asks: sannyāsa or tyāga? 18.1–18.4

Arjuna asks the difference between renunciation and abandonment; Kṛṣṇa distinguishes sannyāsa from tyāga, and declares that tyāga is of three kinds.

18.1Arjuna asks: what is renunciation?
I wish to know the truth of renunciation, O mighty-armed one, and of abandonment, O Hṛṣīkeśa, and the difference between the two, O slayer of Keśin.
18.2Giving up desire, and giving up fruit
The wise call the giving up of desired actions renunciation; the abandonment of all fruits of action the wise call tyāga.
18.3The schools of thought
Some wise men say action is to be given up as evil; others say sacrifice, gift and austerity are not to be given up.
18.4Tyāga is of three kinds
Hear my certain conclusion on tyāga, O best of the Bhāratas; for tyāga, O tiger among men, is declared to be of three kinds.

Sacrifice, gift and austerity must not be dropped 18.5–18.6

Kṛṣṇa's ruling before the classification: these three purify even the wise and are never to be abandoned — but perform them giving up attachment and fruit.

18.5Sacrifice, gift and austerity are not to be given up
The acts of sacrifice, gift and austerity are not to be given up; they must be performed, for they purify even the wise.
18.6Without clinging to fruit
But even these actions should be performed, giving up attachment and the fruits, O Pārtha — this is my certain and best conviction.

The three kinds of abandonment 18.7–18.12

Having ruled that action must not be dropped, Kṛṣṇa classifies the dropping of it: through delusion is tāmasic, from fear of pain rājasic, and giving up only attachment and fruit is sāttvic.

18.7Giving up duty through delusion is tāmasic
The renunciation of prescribed action is not proper; abandoning it through delusion is declared tāmasic.
18.8Giving it up from fear of pain is rājasic
He who gives up action because it brings pain, from fear of bodily suffering — performing the rājasic abandonment, he does not gain the fruit of renunciation.
18.9Doing it because it ought to be done
But the prescribed action done because it ought to be done, O Arjuna, giving up attachment and fruit — that is deemed sāttvic abandonment.
18.10Neither dislikes nor craves
The sāttvic renouncer, endowed with sattva and firm understanding, neither hates unpleasant action nor clings to pleasant.
18.11No one can give up all action
Indeed, the embodied one cannot give up all actions entirely; but he who gives up the fruit of action is called a true renouncer.
18.12Three kinds of fruit
The threefold fruit of action — pleasant, unpleasant and mixed — comes after death to the non-renouncer; but never to the renouncer.

The Five Causes of Action 18.13–18.18

All action is accomplished by five causes: the body, the agent, the instruments, the efforts, and destiny.

18.13Learn the five causes
Learn from me, O mighty-armed one, these five causes of all action, declared in the philosophy of the end of action:
18.14Fate as the fifth
— the seat, the agent, the instruments, the various efforts, and destiny, the fifth.
18.15Whatever action a man undertakes
Whatever action a man undertakes with body, speech or mind — rightly or wrongly — these five are its causes.
18.16Who sees the Self alone as doer sees wrongly
That being so, he who — with imperfect understanding — sees the Self alone as the agent, does not truly see.
18.17Free of ego, he slays and is not bound
He whose mind is free from ego, whose intellect is not stained — though he slays these worlds, he slays not, nor is bound.
18.18Knowledge, action, agent
Knowledge, the known and the knower — the threefold impulse to action; the instrument, the act and the agent — the threefold basis of action.

The guṇas divide knowledge itself 18.19–18.22

Knowledge, action and agent are threefold by the guṇas — hear how they are distinguished.

18.19The threefold knowledge
Knowledge, action and agent are declared threefold by the distinction of the guṇas — hear them as they are in the enumeration of the guṇas.
18.20The Sāttvic knowledge
That knowledge by which one sees one imperishable being in all beings, undivided in the divided — know that as sāttvic.
18.21Rājasic knowledge sees beings as separate
But the knowledge that sees manifold and separate beings in all beings — know that as rājasic.
18.22Irrational, clinging to a part
And the knowledge that clings to one single effect as if it were the whole, without reason, without truth, trivial — is declared tāmasic.

The three kinds of action 18.23–18.25

Action itself divided by the guṇas: sāttvic is done as duty without craving its fruit; rājasic with great effort and ego; tāmasic begun in delusion, heedless of ruin.

18.23Sāttvic action seeks no fruit
Action done as prescribed, without attachment, without desire or aversion, by one who seeks no fruit — is declared sāttvic.
18.24Rājasic action seeks desire
But action done with great effort, seeking the fulfilment of desires, full of ego — is declared rājasic.
18.25Done in delusion
Action begun through delusion, heedless of consequence, of ruin and injury, and of one's own capacity — is declared tāmasic.

The three kinds of doer 18.26–18.28

18.27The rājasic agent
The agent full of passion, longing for the fruit of action, greedy, cruel, impure, moved by joy and sorrow — is declared rājasic.
18.28Deceitful, lazy, stubborn
The agent undisciplined, vulgar, obstinate, deceitful, malicious, lazy, despondent and procrastinating — is declared tāmasic.

Three kinds of happiness 18.36–18.39

Happiness is threefold: the sāttvic, sweet like poison at first and nectar at the end; the rājasic, nectar at first and poison at the end; the tāmasic, born of sleep and sloth.

18.37Nectar at last
That which is like poison at first and nectar at the end — born of the clarity of the Self — is declared sāttvic happiness.
18.38Sweet at first, poison at last
The happiness from the contact of the senses with their objects, nectar at first and poison at the end — is declared rājasic.
18.39Stupefies from beginning
The happiness that deludes the soul at first and at the end, born of sleep, sloth and heedlessness — is declared tāmasic.

This is not to be spoken to one without austerity, nor to the faithless, nor to one who does not serve, nor to one who reviles me.“Do not teach this to one who lacks discipline, to the faithless, to the unwilling, or to one who despises me.”
यो कुरा न तपस्या बिनाकोलाई भन्नुपर्छ, न श्रद्धारहितलाई, न सेवा नगर्नेलाई, न मलाई निन्दा गर्नेलाई।
यह बात न तपस्या-रहित को कहनी चाहिए, न श्रद्धा-रहित को, न सेवा न करने वाले को, न मेरी निन्दा करने वाले को।
18.68 ↗
य इमं परमं गुह्यं मद्भक्तेष्वभिधास्यति । भक्तिं मयि परां कृत्वा मामेवैष्यत्यसंशयः
ya imaṃ paramaṃ guhyaṃ madbhakteṣvabhidhāsyati । bhaktiṃ mayi parāṃ kṛtvā māmevaiṣyatyasaṃśayaḥ
He who teaches this supreme secret to my devotees with supreme devotion — having given me his highest love, he shall come to me.“One who shares this supreme secret with my devotees, out of love for me — giving me his deepest devotion — will reach me.”
जसले मेरा भक्तहरूलाई परम भक्तिसाथ यो परम गोप्य सिकाउँछ — मलाई आफ्नो उच्चतम प्रेम दिएर — ऊ मकहाँ आउनेछ।
जो मेरे भक्तों को परम भक्ति से यह परम गुह्य सिखाता है — मुझे अपना उच्चतम प्रेम देकर — वह मुझे प्राप्त होगा।
18.69 ↗
न च तस्मान्मनुष्येषु कश्चिन्मे प्रियकृत्तमः । भविता न च मे तस्मादन्यः प्रियतरो भुवि
na ca tasmānmanuṣyeṣu kaścinme priyakṛttamaḥ । bhavitā na ca me tasmādanyaḥ priyataro bhuvi
None among men does me greater service than he; and none shall be dearer to me on earth than he.“No one serves me more than such a teacher; no one will be dearer to me than he.”
उसभन्दा बढी मेरो सेवा गर्ने मानिसहरूमा कोही छैन; र पृथ्वीमा उसभन्दा मलाई प्रिय कोही हुनेछैन।
उससे बढ़कर मेरी सेवा करने वाला मनुष्यों में कोई नहीं; और पृथ्वी पर उससे मुझे प्रिय कोई नहीं होगा।
18.70 ↗
अध्येष्यते च य इमं धर्म्यं संवादमावयोः । ज्ञानयज्ञेन तेनाहमिष्टः स्यामिति मे मतिः
adhyeṣyate ca ya imaṃ dharmyaṃ saṃvādamāvayoḥ । jñānayajñena tenāhamiṣṭaḥ syāmiti me matiḥ
And he who studies this sacred dialogue of ours — by him I am worshipped with the sacrifice of knowledge; so I deem.“And whoever studies this holy dialogue of ours — I deem him to have worshipped me with the sacrifice of wisdom.”
र जसले हाम्रो यो पवित्र संवादको अध्ययन गर्छ — उसद्वारा म ज्ञानयज्ञले पूजित हुन्छु; यो मेरो विश्वास हो।
और जो हमारे इस पवित्र संवाद का अध्ययन करता है — उससे मैं ज्ञान-यज्ञ से पूजित होता हूँ; यह मेरा विश्वास है।
18.71 ↗
श्रद्धावाननसूयश्च शृणुयादपि यो नरः । सोऽपि मुक्तः शुभांल्लोकान्प्राप्नुयात्पुण्यकर्मणाम्
śraddhāvānanasūyaśca śṛṇuyādapi yo naraḥ । so’pi muktaḥ śubhāṃllokānprāpnuyātpuṇyakarmaṇām
Even the man who hears it with faith, without carping — freed, he attains the auspicious worlds of the virtuous.“Even one who merely listens with faith and without envy is released and reaches the blissful worlds of the righteous.”
श्रद्धाले, निन्दा बिना, यसलाई सुन्ने मानिस पनि — मुक्त भएर — सत्पुरुषहरूका शुभ लोकहरूमा पुग्छ।
श्रद्धा से, बिना निन्दा के, इसे सुनने वाला मनुष्य भी — मुक्त होकर — सत्पुरुषों के शुभ लोकों को प्राप्त होता है।

Arjuna answers: I will do your word 18.72–18.73

Kṛṣṇa's last question and the answer the whole Gītā was spoken to produce: the delusion is destroyed, memory regained, doubt gone — I will do your word.

18.72The Lord's question
Has this been heard by you, O Pārtha, with a one-pointed mind? Has the delusion of ignorance been destroyed?
18.73My delusion is gone; I will do your word
Destroyed is my delusion; I have gained memory by your grace, O Acyuta; I stand firm, my doubt gone — I will do your word.

18.72 ↗
कच्चिदेतच्छ्रुतं पार्थ त्वयैकाग्रेण चेतसा । कच्चिदज्ञानसंमोहः प्रणष्टस्ते धनंजय
kaccidetacchrutaṃ pārtha tvayaikāgreṇa cetasā । kaccidajñānasaṃmohaḥ praṇaṣṭaste dhanaṃjaya
Has this been heard by you, O Pārtha, with a one-pointed mind? Has the delusion of ignorance been destroyed?“Did you hear this with full attention, Arjuna? Has the confusion of ignorance been dispelled?”
हे पार्थ, के यो तिमीले एकाग्र मनले सुन्यौ? के अज्ञानको मोह नष्ट भयो?
हे पार्थ, क्या यह तूने एकाग्र मन से सुना? क्या अज्ञान का मोह नष्ट हुआ?
18.73 ↗
अर्जुन उवाच । नष्टो मोहः स्मृतिर्लब्धा त्वत्प्रसादान्मयाच्युत । स्थितोऽस्मि गतसंदेहः करिष्ये वचनं तव
arjuna uvāca । naṣṭo mohaḥ smṛtirlabdhā tvatprasādānmayācyuta । sthito’smi gatasaṃdehaḥ kariṣye vacanaṃ tava
Arjuna said: Destroyed is my delusion; I have gained memory by your grace, O Acyuta; I stand firm, my doubt gone — I will do your word.Arjuna replies: “My delusion is gone; by your grace I have regained memory, O changeless Lord. I stand firm, free of doubt — I will follow your command.”
अर्जुनले भने: मेरो मोह नष्ट भयो; हे अच्युत, तपाईंको कृपाले मैले स्मृति पाएँ; म स्थिर छु, मेरो सन्देह गयो — म तपाईंको आज्ञा पालन गर्नेछु।
अर्जुन ने कहा: मेरा मोह नष्ट हो गया; हे अच्युत, आपकी कृपा से मुझे स्मृति मिली; मैं स्थिर हूँ, मेरा सन्देह गया — मैं आपकी आज्ञा का पालन करूँगा।

Sañjaya tells what he heard 18.74–18.77

The narrator steps forward: by Vyāsa's grace he heard this supreme secret from the Lord of yoga himself, and remembering the dialogue and that marvellous form, he rejoices again and again.

18.74Thus I heard this wondrous dialogue
Thus I heard the wondrous, thrilling dialogue of Vāsudeva and the great-souled Pārtha.
18.75The grace of Vyāsa
By the grace of Vyāsa I heard this supreme, most secret yoga from Kṛṣṇa, the Lord of yoga, speaking himself.
18.76The joy of remembering
Remembering again and again this wondrous, holy dialogue of Keśava and Arjuna, O king, I rejoice again and again.
18.77Remembering that form, I rejoice again
And remembering again and again that most wondrous form of Hari, my wonder is great, O king — and I rejoice again and again.

18.74 ↗
सञ्जय उवाच । इत्यहं वासुदेवस्य पार्थस्य च महात्मनः । संवादमिममश्रौषमद्भुतं रोमहर्षणम्
sañjaya uvāca । ityahaṃ vāsudevasya pārthasya ca mahātmanaḥ । saṃvādamimamaśrauṣamadbhutaṃ romaharṣaṇam
Sañjaya said: Thus I heard the wondrous, thrilling dialogue of Vāsudeva and the great-souled Pārtha.Sañjaya narrates: “Thus I heard the wonderful, hair-raising conversation of Vāsudeva and the noble Pārtha.”
सञ्जयले भने: यसरी मैले वासुदेव र महात्मा पार्थको अद्भुत, रोमाञ्चक संवाद सुनेँ।
संजय ने कहा: इस प्रकार मैंने वासुदेव और महात्मा पार्थ का अद्भुत, रोमांचक संवाद सुना।
18.75 ↗
व्यासप्रसादाच्छ्रुतवानिमं गुह्यतमं परम् । योगं योगेश्वरात्कृष्णात्साक्षात्कथयतः स्वयम्
vyāsaprasādācchrutavānimaṃ guhyatamaṃ param । yogaṃ yogeśvarātkṛṣṇātsākṣātkathayataḥ svayam
By the grace of Vyāsa I heard this supreme, most secret yoga from Kṛṣṇa, the Lord of yoga, speaking himself.“By Vyāsa's grace I heard this supreme, secret yoga from Kṛṣṇa himself, the master of yoga.”
व्यासको कृपाले मैले यो परम, सबैभन्दा गोप्य योग — योगेश्वर कृष्ण आफैं बोलेको — सुनेँ।
व्यास की कृपा से मैंने यह परम, सबसे गुह्य योग — योगेश्वर कृष्ण स्वयं बोले — सुना।
18.76 ↗
राजन् संस्मृत्य संस्मृत्य संवादमिममद्भुतम् । केशवार्जुनयोः पुण्यं हृष्यामि च मुहुर्मुहुः
rājan saṃsmṛtya saṃsmṛtya saṃvādamimamadbhutam । keśavārjunayoḥ puṇyaṃ hṛṣyāmi ca muhurmuhuḥ
Remembering again and again this wondrous, holy dialogue of Keśava and Arjuna, O king, I rejoice again and again.“Recalling again and again this wonderful and sacred dialogue between Keśava and Arjuna, O king, I thrill with joy.”
हे राजन्, केशव र अर्जुनको यो अद्भुत, पवित्र संवाद बारम्बार सम्झेर, म बारम्बार रमाउँछु।
हे राजन्, केशव और अर्जुन के इस अद्भुत, पवित्र संवाद को बार-बार स्मरण करके, मैं बार-बार हर्षित होता हूँ।
18.77 ↗
तच्च संस्मृत्य संस्मृत्य रूपमत्यद्भुतं हरेः । विस्मयो मे महान्राजन् हृष्यामि च पुनः पुनः
tacca saṃsmṛtya saṃsmṛtya rūpamatyadbhutaṃ hareḥ । vismayo me mahānrājan hṛṣyāmi ca punaḥ punaḥ
And remembering again and again that most wondrous form of Hari, my wonder is great, O king — and I rejoice again and again.“And remembering, again and again, that most marvellous form of the Lord, I am filled with wonder and delight.”
र हे राजन्, हरिको त्यो सबैभन्दा अद्भुत रूप बारम्बार सम्झेर, मेरो आश्चर्य ठूलो छ — र म बारम्बार रमाउँछु।
और हे राजन्, हरि के उस अत्यन्त अद्भुत रूप को बार-बार स्मरण करके, मेरा आश्चर्य बड़ा है — और मैं बार-बार हर्षित होता हूँ।

Where Kṛṣṇa and Arjuna are, there is victory 18.78

The final verse of the Gītā — Sañjaya's benediction: wherever the Lord of yoga and the archer stand together, there are prosperity, victory, glory and steadfast wisdom.

Wherever Kṛṣṇa, the Lord of yoga, and Pārtha, the archer, are — there are prosperity, victory, glory and constant wisdom; so I deem.
