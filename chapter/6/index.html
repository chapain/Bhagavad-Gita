Chapter 6 — Dhyāna Yoga

The true sannyāsin renounces the fruit6.1–6.4

Renunciation is not the abandoning of fire and rites but of the fruit of action; no one becomes a yogi without giving up selfish intention.

6.1Not he who lights no fire
He who does his duty without depending on the fruit is the sannyāsin and the yogi — not he who lights no fire and does no rites.
6.2Renunciation is Yoga
Know that what they call renunciation is yoga; no one becomes a yogi who has not renounced selfish intention.
6.3Action for the ascending, stillness for the ascended
For the sage who wishes to ascend to yoga, action is the means; for one who has ascended, serenity is the means.
6.4Clinging to nothing, he has risen in yoga
When he clings neither to sense-objects nor to actions, having renounced all intention, then he is said to have ascended to yoga.

Lift the Self by the Self6.5–6.9

The Self is the friend of the self and also its enemy; for the conquered self, it is the best of friends — for the unconquered, hostile.

6.5The Self is the friend and the enemy of the Self
Let a man lift himself by his own Self, and not degrade himself; the Self alone is the friend of the self, and the Self alone its enemy. For him who has conquered the self by the Self, the Self is a friend — but for the unconquered, it remains hostile like an enemy.
6.6The Self is the enemy
Master yourself and the Self befriends you; fail to master yourself and the Self turns hostile.
6.7The self-conquered is at peace
The self-conquered and peaceful man is settled in the supreme Self, alike in cold and heat, pleasure and pain, honour and dishonour.
6.8A clod, a stone, and gold alike
The yogi satisfied with knowledge and realization, unmoved, master of the senses, is said to be united — to whom a clod, a stone and gold are the same.
6.9Equal to friend and foe
He excels who is equal-minded toward well-wishers, friends, enemies, the indifferent, the hateful, kinsmen, the righteous and the sinful alike.

The Seat of Meditation6.10–6.15

The practical instructions: a solitary place, a firm seat neither too high nor too low, the body held erect and still.

6.10Alone, in solitude
Let the yogi constantly discipline himself, remaining in solitude, alone, with mind and body controlled, free from desire and possessions.
6.11A clean place, neither too high nor too low
In a clean place, having set a firm seat neither too high nor too low, covered with cloth, deerskin and kuśa grass — there, seated, making the mind one-pointed, with thought and senses controlled, let him practise yoga for self-purification.
6.12Calm the mind and senses
Sitting there, focus the mind, calm thought and senses, and practise yoga to purify yourself.
6.13Body, head and neck erect
Holding body, head and neck erect and still, gazing at the tip of his own nose without looking around — serene, fearless, firm in the vow of celibacy, controlling the mind, let him sit intent on me.
6.14Calm and fearless
Calm and fearless, chaste and self-controlled, thoughts on me alone — sit in meditation, devoted to me.
6.15The yogi attains the peace that rests in me
Thus ever disciplining himself, the yogi of subdued mind attains the peace abiding in me, which culminates in nirvāṇa.

Yoga is for one who lives in measure6.16–6.17

Yoga is not for the one who eats too much or too little, sleeps too much or too little — it is the middle way.

6.16Not for him who eats too much
Yoga is not for him who eats too much, nor for him who does not eat at all; not for him who sleeps too much, nor for him who keeps awake.
6.17Moderate in food, sleep and work
For him who is moderate in food and recreation, moderate in effort in action, moderate in sleep and waking — yoga becomes the destroyer of sorrow.

The Lamp in a Windless Place6.18–6.23

When the disciplined mind rests in the Self alone, it is like a lamp in a windless place that does not flicker.

6.18When the mind rests in the Self
When the well-controlled mind rests in the Self alone, free from longing for all desires, then one is said to be united.
6.19As a lamp in a windless place
As a lamp in a windless place does not flicker — that is the figure used of the yogi of controlled mind, practising union with the Self.
6.20Beholding the Self, content in the Self
That in which the mind, restrained by the practice of yoga, comes to rest, and in which, beholding the Self by the self, one is content in the Self — that boundless joy, grasped by the intellect beyond the senses, established in which one never departs from the truth —
6.21A joy the senses cannot reach
— when you taste that boundless joy known beyond the senses, and nothing can shake you from it —
6.22No gain is counted greater than this
— having gained which one thinks no other gain greater, and established in which one is not shaken even by heavy sorrow.
6.23Practise with undiscouraged mind
Know that severance from union with pain is called yoga; that yoga is to be practised with determination and an undespairing mind.

Bringing the Mind Home6.24–6.26

Abandon every desire born of intention; then, slowly and by degrees, let the mind come to rest — and wherever it wanders, bring it back.

6.25Slowly, gradually, let him come to rest
— little by little let him come to rest, with intellect held firm; fixing the mind on the Self, let him think of nothing at all.
6.26Wherever the mind wanders, bring it back
From whatever cause the restless, unsteady mind wanders away, from that let him restrain it and bring it back under the control of the Self.

Seeing the Self in All6.27–6.32

Supreme bliss comes to the stainless yogi, who sees the Self in all beings and all beings in the Self — and so is never lost to me.

6.27Supreme bliss comes to the stainless
Supreme bliss comes to the yogi whose mind is at peace, whose passion is stilled, who is stainless and has become Brahman. Thus constantly disciplining himself, freed from stain, he easily attains the boundless joy of contact with Brahman.
6.28The purified reach bliss
Practising this always, the purified yogi easily reaches the limitless joy of union with Brahman.
6.29The Self in all beings — And I am never lost to him
Seeing the Self in all beings and all beings in the Self, he sees the same everywhere. He who sees me everywhere and sees everything in me — for him I am never lost, nor is he lost to me.
6.30I am never lost to him
Whoever sees me in all and all in me — I never leave him, and he never leaves me.
6.31The supreme yogi
Established in unity, the yogi who worships me abiding in all beings dwells in me, whatever his mode of life. He who, by the likeness of his own self, sees the same everywhere — in pleasure and in pain — is deemed the supreme yogi.
6.32He who feels all as his own
One who feels others' joy and pain as his own, seeing all equally — he is the greatest yogi, Arjuna.

Arjuna says: the mind is restless as wind6.33–6.36

Arjuna objects that the mind is as hard to hold as the wind; Kṛṣṇa answers with practice and dispassion.

6.33Arjuna: the mind is too restless for this
This yoga of evenness you have declared — I do not see its steady continuance, because of restlessness.
6.34The mind is restless as the wind
The mind is restless, turbulent, powerful and obstinate; to restrain it seems to me as hard as to restrain the wind.
6.35By practice and detachment
Undoubtedly the mind is hard to curb and restless — but by practice and by dispassion it can be held.
6.36For the unrestrained, Yoga is hard
Yoga is hard to attain for one whose self is unrestrained; but for the self-controlled, striving by right means, it can be won.

अर्जुन उवाच । योऽयं योगस्त्वया प्रोक्तः साम्येन मधुसूदन । एतस्याहं न पश्यामि चञ्चलत्वात्स्थितिं स्थिराम्
arjuna uvāca । yo’yaṃ yogastvayā proktaḥ sāmyena madhusūdana । etasyāhaṃ na paśyāmi cañcalatvātsthitiṃ sthirām
Arjuna said: This yoga of equanimity declared by you, O Madhusūdana — I see no firm foundation for it, because of restlessness.Arjuna asks: “This yoga of even-mindedness you describe — I don't see how it can last, since the mind is so restless.”
अर्जुनले भने: हे मधुसूदन, तपाईंले बताउनुभएको यो समत्वयोग — चञ्चलताका कारण यसको कुनै स्थिर आधार म देख्दिनँ।
अर्जुन ने कहा: हे मधुसूदन, आपके द्वारा कहा गया यह समत्व-योग — चंचलता के कारण मैं इसकी कोई स्थिर नींव नहीं देखता।
6.34 ↗
चञ्चलं हि मनः कृष्ण प्रमाथि बलवद्दृढम् । तस्याहं निग्रहं मन्ये वायोरिव सुदुष्करम्
cañcalaṃ hi manaḥ kṛṣṇa pramāthi balavaddṛḍham । tasyāhaṃ nigrahaṃ manye vāyoriva suduṣkaram
Verily the mind is restless, turbulent, strong and unyielding, O Kṛṣṇa; I think it is as hard to restrain as the wind.“The mind is wild, turbulent, stubborn, and strong — I think controlling it is as hard as catching the wind.”
हे कृष्ण, मन निश्चय नै चञ्चल, आवेगी, बलिष्ठ र अडिग छ; मलाई लाग्छ यसलाई रोक्नु हावा रोक्नुजस्तै कठिन छ।
हे कृष्ण, मन निश्चय ही चंचल, आवेगी, बलवान् और अडिग है; मैं समझता हूँ कि इसे रोकना वायु को रोकने के समान कठिन है।
6.35 ↗
श्रीभगवानुवाच । असंशयं महाबाहो मनो दुर्निग्रहं चलम् । अभ्यासेन तु कौन्तेय वैराग्येण च गृह्यते
śrībhagavānuvāca । asaṃśayaṃ mahābāho mano durnigrahaṃ calam । abhyāsena tu kaunteya vairāgyeṇa ca gṛhyate
The Blessed Lord said: Undoubtedly the mind is restless and hard to restrain, O mighty-armed one; but it is restrained, O son of Kuntī, by practice and by dispassion.Kṛṣṇa said: “Yes, the mind is restless and hard to tame — but practice and detachment can master it.”
श्रीभगवान्ले भन्नुभयो: हे महाबाहो, मन चञ्चल र रोक्न कठिन छ भन्ने निश्चय हो; तर हे कुन्तीपुत्र, अभ्यास र वैराग्यद्वारा यसलाई रोक्न सकिन्छ।
श्री भगवान् ने कहा: हे महाबाहो, मन चंचल और रोकने में कठिन है — इसमें सन्देह नहीं; किन्तु हे कुन्तीपुत्र, अभ्यास और वैराग्य से इसे रोका जा सकता है।
6.36 ↗
असंयतात्मना योगो दुष्प्राप इति मे मतिः । वश्यात्मना तु यतता शक्योऽवाप्तुमुपायतः
asaṃyatātmanā yogo duṣprāpa iti me matiḥ । vaśyātmanā tu yatatā śakyo’vāptumupāyataḥ
Yoga is hard to attain, I think, for one whose self is unrestrained; but for the self-controlled one who strives by the proper means, it is attainable.“Without self-mastery, yoga is very hard; but one who is self-controlled and strives rightly will attain it.”
आत्मा संयत नभएकाका निम्ति योग पाउन गाह्रो छ भन्ने मेरो धारणा छ; तर आत्मसंयमी, उचित साधनले प्रयत्न गर्नेले यसलाई पाउन सक्छ।
जिसने आत्मा को वश में नहीं किया, उसके लिए योग कठिन है — मेरा यह मत है; किन्तु आत्म-संयमी, उचित साधन से प्रयत्न करने वाला इसे प्राप्त कर सकता है।

The Yogi Who Falls Away6.37–6.43

What becomes of one who strives but fails? He is not destroyed — no one who does good ever comes to a bad end.

6.37Arjuna asks what befalls one who fails
He who has faith but is not persevering, whose mind falls away from yoga and fails to reach perfection — what path does he take? Fallen from both, without support, does he not perish like a scattered cloud on the path to Brahman?
6.38Lost like a torn cloud
Lost from both worlds — does he perish like a torn cloud, clinging to nothing, confused on the path to Brahman?
6.39No one who does good comes to grief
You alone can dispel this doubt of mine completely; none but you can be found to remove it. Neither in this world nor the next is there destruction for him; no one who does good ever comes to a bad end.
6.40Never comes to grief
Kṛṣṇa said: “Never, Arjuna — neither here nor after death — does such a one perish. No one who does good ever goes to evil.
6.41Born again in a pure house
Having reached the worlds of the righteous and dwelt there many years, the fallen yogi is born in the house of the pure and prosperous.
6.42Or in a family of yogis
Or he is born in a family of wise yogis — though such a birth is very hard to gain in this world.
6.43He regains the knowledge of his former birth
There he regains the knowledge acquired in his former body, and strives again for perfection, O joy of the Kurus.

6.37 ↗
अर्जुन उवाच । अयतिः श्रद्धयोपेतो योगाच्चलितमानसः । अप्राप्य योगसंसिद्धिं कां गतिं कृष्ण गच्छति
arjuna uvāca । ayatiḥ śraddhayopeto yogāccalitamānasaḥ । aprāpya yogasaṃsiddhiṃ kāṃ gatiṃ kṛṣṇa gacchati
Arjuna said: He who, full of faith but unable to control himself, whose mind has fallen away from yoga — not attaining perfection in yoga — what end does he reach, O Kṛṣṇa?Arjuna asks: “What happens to someone who tries, with faith, but fails — whose mind falls from yoga before reaching perfection, Kṛṣṇa?”
अर्जुनले भने: हे कृष्ण, जो श्रद्धाले भरिएको तर आफूलाई नियन्त्रण गर्न नसकेको, जसको मन योगबाट च्युत भएको छ — योगमा सिद्धि नपाई — त्यो के गति पाउँछ?
अर्जुन ने कहा: हे कृष्ण, जो श्रद्धा से भरा पर स्वयं को वश में न कर सका, जिसका मन योग से च्युत हो गया — योग में सिद्धि न पाकर — वह किस गति को प्राप्त होता है?
6.38 ↗
कच्चिन्नोभयविभ्रष्टश्छिन्नाभ्रमिव नश्यति । अप्रतिष्ठो महाबाहो विमूढो ब्रह्मणः पथि
kaccinnobhayavibhraṣṭaśchinnābhramiva naśyati । apratiṣṭho mahābāho vimūḍho brahmaṇaḥ pathi
Falling from both, does he not perish like a riven cloud, O mighty-armed one, without support, deluded on the path of Brahman?“Lost from both worlds — does he perish like a torn cloud, clinging to nothing, confused on the path to Brahman?”
हे महाबाहो, दुवैबाट च्युत भएर, आधार बिना, ब्रह्मको मार्गमा मोहित भएर — ऊ च्यातिएको बादलझैं नष्ट हुँदैन र?
हे महाबाहो, दोनों से च्युत होकर, बिना आधार, ब्रह्म के मार्ग में मोहित होकर — क्या वह छिन्न बादल के समान नष्ट नहीं होता?
6.39 ↗
एतन्मे संशयं कृष्ण छेत्तुमर्हस्यशेषतः । त्वदन्यः संशयस्यास्य छेत्ता न ह्युपपद्यते
etanme saṃśayaṃ kṛṣṇa chettumarhasyaśeṣataḥ । tvadanyaḥ saṃśayasyāsya chettā na hyupapadyate
This doubt of mine you should dispel wholly, O Kṛṣṇa; no other than you is found to destroy this doubt.“Please remove this doubt of mine completely, Kṛṣṇa — no one else can resolve it.”
हे कृष्ण, तपाईंले नै मेरो यो सन्देह पूर्ण रूपमा मेट्नुपर्छ; यो सन्देह नष्ट गर्ने तपाईंबाहेक अरू कोही देखिँदैन।
हे कृष्ण, आपको ही मेरा यह सन्देह पूर्णतः दूर करना चाहिए; इस सन्देह को नष्ट करने वाला आपके अतिरिक्त और कोई नहीं दिखता।
6.40 ↗
श्रीभगवानुवाच । पार्थ नैवेह नामुत्र विनाशस्तस्य विद्यते । न हि कल्याणकृत्कश्चिद्दुर्गतिं तात गच्छति
śrībhagavānuvāca । pārtha naiveha nāmutra vināśastasya vidyate । na hi kalyāṇakṛtkaściddurgatiṃ tāta gacchati
The Blessed Lord said: O Pārtha, neither in this world nor in the next is there destruction for him; for none who does good, my son, goes to evil.Kṛṣṇa said: “Never, Arjuna — neither here nor after death — does such a one perish. No one who does good comes to a bad end.”
श्रीभगवान्ले भन्नुभयो: हे पार्थ, न यस लोकमा न परलोकमा उसको विनाश हुन्छ; किनकि हे पुत्र, असल कर्म गर्ने कोही पनि दुर्गतिमा जाँदैन।
श्री भगवान् ने कहा: हे पार्थ, न इस लोक में न परलोक में उसका विनाश होता है; क्योंकि हे पुत्र, शुभ कर्म करने वाला कोई दुर्गति को नहीं जाता।
6.41 ↗
प्राप्य पुण्यकृतां लोकानुषित्वा शाश्वतीः समाः । शुचीनां श्रीमतां गेहे योगभ्रष्टोऽभिजायते
prāpya puṇyakṛtāṃ lokānuṣitvā śāśvatīḥ samāḥ । śucīnāṃ śrīmatāṃ gehe yogabhraṣṭo’bhijāyate
Having attained the worlds of the righteous and dwelt there for immemorial years, he who fell from yoga is born again in the house of the pure and prosperous.“He enjoys the realms of the virtuous for ages, then is reborn in a pure and fortunate family.”
पुण्यकर्मीहरूका लोक प्राप्त गरेर, त्यहाँ असंख्य वर्ष बिताएर, योगबाट च्युत व्यक्ति शुद्ध र समृद्ध घरमा फेरि जन्मिन्छ।
पुण्य-कर्मियों के लोकों को प्राप्त कर, वहाँ असंख्य वर्षों तक निवास कर, योग से च्युत पुरुष शुद्ध और समृद्ध घर में फिर जन्म लेता है।
6.42 ↗
अथवा योगिनामेव कुले भवति धीमताम् । एतद्धि दुर्लभतरं लोके जन्म यदीदृशम्
athavā yogināmeva kule bhavati dhīmatām । etaddhi durlabhataraṃ loke janma yadīdṛśam
Or he is born into a family of wise yogis; truly a birth like this is very hard to obtain in this world.“Or else he is born into a household of seasoned yogis — and that is the rarest birth of all.”
वा ऊ बुद्धिमान् योगीहरूको घरमा जन्मिन्छ; साँच्चै, यस्तो जन्म यस संसारमा पाउन धेरै कठिन छ।
अथवा वह बुद्धिमान् योगियों के घर में जन्म लेता है; सचमुच, ऐसा जन्म इस लोक में बहुत कठिनता से मिलता है।
6.43 ↗
तत्र तं बुद्धिसंयोगं लभते पौर्वदेहिकम् । यतते च ततो भूयः संसिद्धौ कुरुनन्दन
tatra taṃ buddhisaṃyogaṃ labhate paurvadehikam । yatate ca tato bhūyaḥ saṃsiddhau kurunandana
There he regains the understanding of his former body, O son of the Kurus, and from there he strives again for perfection.“There the wisdom of his past life returns, and from that point he presses on toward perfection.”
हे कुरुनन्दन, त्यहाँ ऊ आफ्नो पूर्वशरीरको बुद्धि फेरि प्राप्त गर्छ, र त्यहीँबाट सिद्धिका निम्ति फेरि प्रयत्न गर्छ।
हे कुरुनन्दन, वहाँ वह अपने पूर्व-शरीर की बुद्धि को फिर प्राप्त करता है, और वहीं से सिद्धि के लिए फिर प्रयत्न करता है।

The greatest yogi abides in me6.44–6.47

Carried on by former practice, the yogi is purified over many births — and the greatest yogi of all is he who worships me with faith.

6.44Carried onward by former practice
By that former practice alone he is carried on, even against his will; even the seeker after yoga goes beyond the word of the Vedas.
6.45Perfected through many births
The yogi who strives with effort, purified of sin and perfected through many births, then reaches the supreme goal.
6.46The yogi is greater than the ascetic
The yogi is greater than the ascetic, greater even than the man of knowledge, greater than the ritualist — therefore become a yogi, O Arjuna.
6.47Dearest of all: He who worships me
And of all yogis, he who worships me with faith, his inmost self absorbed in me — he is deemed by me the most devoted of all.

6.44 ↗
पूर्वाभ्यासेन तेनैव ह्रियते ह्यवशोऽपि सः । जिज्ञासुरपि योगस्य शब्दब्रह्मातिवर्तते
pūrvābhyāsena tenaiva hriyate hyavaśo’pi saḥ । jijñāsurapi yogasya śabdabrahmātivartate
By that very former practice he is carried along, even in spite of himself; even the one who merely wishes to know yoga transcends the mere word of the Vedas.“His past practice carries him forward even against his will — even wanting to know yoga takes you beyond mere scripture.”
त्यही पूर्वाभ्यासले गर्दा ऊ अनिच्छाले पनि अघि बढ्छ; योग जान्न चाहने मात्रले पनि वेदको शब्दभन्दा माथि उक्लन्छ।
उसी पूर्व-अभ्यास से वह अनिच्छा से भी आगे बढ़ता है; योग को जानने की इच्छा मात्र करने वाला भी वेद के शब्द से परे चला जाता है।
6.45 ↗
प्रयत्नाद्यतमानस्तु योगी संशुद्धकिल्बिषः । अनेकजन्मसंसिद्धस्ततो याति परां गतिम्
prayatnādyatamānastu yogī saṃśuddhakilbiṣaḥ । anekajanmasaṃsiddhastato yāti parāṃ gatim

किन्तु प्रयत्नपूर्वक अभ्यास करने वाला, पाप से शुद्ध, अनेक जन्मों से क्रमशः सिद्ध हुआ योगी तब परम अवस्था को प्राप्त होता है।
6.46 ↗
तपस्विभ्योऽधिको योगी ज्ञानिभ्योऽपि मतोऽधिकः । कर्मिभ्यश्चाधिको योगी तस्माद्योगी भवार्जुन
tapasvibhyo’dhiko yogī jñānibhyo’pi mato’dhikaḥ । karmibhyaścādhiko yogī tasmādyogī bhavārjuna
The yogi is greater than the ascetic, deemed greater than the man of knowledge, and greater than the man of rites; therefore be a yogi, O Arjuna.“The yogi surpasses the ascetic, the scholar, and the ritualist — so become a yogi, Arjuna.”
योगी तपस्वीभन्दा श्रेष्ठ छ, ज्ञानीभन्दा पनि श्रेष्ठ मानिन्छ, र कर्मकाण्डीभन्दा पनि श्रेष्ठ; त्यसैले हे अर्जुन, योगी बन।
योगी तपस्वी से श्रेष्ठ है, ज्ञानी से भी श्रेष्ठ माना जाता है, और कर्मकाण्डी से भी श्रेष्ठ; इसलिए हे अर्जुन, योगी बन।
6.47 ↗
योगिनामपि सर्वेषां मद्गतेनान्तरात्मना । श्रद्धावान्भजते यो मां स मे युक्ततमो मतः
yogināmapi sarveṣāṃ madgatenāntarātmanā । śraddhāvānbhajate yo māṃ sa me yuktatamo mataḥ
And among all yogis, he who, full of faith, worships me with his inmost self established in me — him I deem the most yoked, in my view.“And among all yogis, the one who worships me with deep faith, his whole being fixed on me — he is the greatest of all, in my eyes.”
र सबै योगीहरूमा, जो श्रद्धाले भरिएर, आफ्नो अन्तरात्मा ममा स्थिर गरेर मेरो पूजा गर्छ — मेरो दृष्टिमा उसैलाई म सबैभन्दा युक्त मान्छु।
और सब योगियों में, जो श्रद्धा से भरकर, अपने अन्तरात्मा को मुझ में स्थिर करके मेरी पूजा करता है — मेरी दृष्टि में उसे ही मैं सबसे युक्त मानता हूँ।
