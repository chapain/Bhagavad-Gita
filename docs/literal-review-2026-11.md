## Findings and whole-book class screens

### 1.45 — added blindness (fixed, ne/hi)

`rājya-sukha-lobhena` says “through greed for kingdom and pleasure”; it does
not say “blinded”. The Nepali and Hindi literals added अन्धा / अंधे. Removed
that addition, preserving the instrumental relation and the rest of the verse.
The English “driven by greed” renders that relation and does not add blindness.

Screened all 700 literals for English blind/blindness and Devanagari अन्ध/अंध.
The other six hits (8.09, 10.11, 13.17, 14.13, 16.22, 18.32) refer to darkness,
not blindness, and each has `tamas` in the Sanskrit. No other blindness hit.
The regression check distinguishes अन्धकार from अन्धा. This lexical screen
cannot rule out other ways to add figurative blindness.

### 2.07 — omitted weakness and defect (fixed, en/hi; ne opening refined)

`kārpaṇya-doṣa-upahata-svabhāvaḥ` has both weakness and defect as well as
impaired nature; confusion about duty is the separate `dharma-sammūḍha-cetāḥ`.
A whole-book scan of word rows for कार्पण्य and उपहत yielded only 2.07 and
1.38. In 1.38 `lobha-upahata-cetasaḥ` is rendered as minds overcome by greed
in all three languages, so the same omission is not present there. This scan
covers those forms, not every possible compound with a similar grammatical
structure. English and Hindi follow the corrected source meaning; the Nepali
opening follows the owner's requested participial construction.

### इव — seeming or comparison omitted (fixed)

Screened every `flow` word row in all 700 verses for इव: 16 verses (1.30,
2.10, 2.58, 2.67, 3.02, 3.36, 5.10, 6.34, 6.38, 7.07, 11.44, 13.16,
15.08, 18.37, 18.38, 18.48). Compared each occurrence with all three literals.
This is an exact-row scan: a missing or differently split word row would not
be found by it.

* 1.30 `bhramatīva` — “the mind seems to reel”, not unqualified reeling;
  fixed in en/ne/hi.
* 3.02 has इव twice: “apparently mixed words” and “you seem to confuse”.
  Nepali had neither qualification; Hindi had the first but not the second.
  Both now use मानौँ/मानो to qualify the sentence. The second word-row gloss
  says “indeed”; that gloss is a separate issue, not silently changed here.
* 18.38 `viṣam iva` — “like poison”; English had a bare metaphor. Added
  “like” for both nectar (उपमम्) and poison; ne/hi already express comparison.

The remaining exact-row occurrences express comparison or seeming in all three
literals. The screen checks this grammatical class only, not complete fidelity
of the 16 verses.

### 2.08 — prosperous kingdom and the conjunction (fixed, all languages)

ऋद्धम् (“prosperous”) was missing. The kingdom and sovereignty over gods
are joined by च, not given as alternatives; all three literals read “or”.
They now keep prosperity, both attainments and अपि (“even”).

A whole-book row-prefix scan for ऋद्ध/ऋद्धि/समृद्ध found 2.08, two repeated
समृद्धवेगाः rows at 11.29, and समृद्धम् at 11.33; the 11.29 literals render
full speed and 11.33 renders a prosperous kingdom. A separate whole-book
screen for च with literal “or”/वा/या and no separate वा/अथवा row flagged
11 verses: 2.08, 2.24, 3.18, 7.12, 9.29, 10.39, 11.24, 11.48, 11.49,
16.07 and 17.28. The other ten were read for this conjunction issue: the
“or” occurs within negative enumerations, inclusive lists or other clauses;
it does not replace the affirmative conjunction joining the two attainments
of 2.08. This lexical screen cannot catch every synonymous conjunction,
mis-split row, or other relational error.

### 2.65 — intellect mistaken for mind (fixed, English)

प्रसन्नचेतसः refers to the serene-minded person; बुद्धिः is the intellect
that becomes steady. English had “the mind of the serene”; ne/hi already have
बुद्धि. The English now distinguishes mind and intellect. Across all 700, a
row-form बुद्धि plus English “mind” without “intellect”, “understanding”,
“wisdom”, “reason”, “intelligence” or “discernment” produced only 2.65. This
limited screen does not establish correctness of every other बुद्धि rendering.

### 3.23 — the independent statement turned into a conditional result

The first clause starts “if I did not engage ...”, continued by 3.24;
मनुष्या ... अनुवर्तन्ते states that people follow his path in the present.
All three literals made that present statement the hypothetical result of the
if-clause. They now preserve the unfinished conditional and independent
statement. An English whole-book exact-row यदि + “if” + “then” screen found
3.23 and 11.04; the latter has a genuine conditional request, so it was not
changed. This is not a comprehensive conditional-logic proof.

### Subsequent corrections in Chapters 1–4

* 1.10: ne/hi “even though” supplied a concession absent from the two parallel
  descriptions; en already reflected the Sanskrit. The exact word-row
  अभिरक्षितम् occurs only in 1.10 across the 700 verses (twice in that verse).
* 1.11: अवस्थिताः is a participle “stationed”, not a separate imperative to
  take positions; “above all” adds a ranking. Corrected all three languages.
  The other exact-row अवस्थिताः occurrences are 1.33, 2.06 and 11.32, where
  the literals describe standing or being arrayed, not an added order.
* 1.13: सहसा “suddenly” was missing in ne/hi; en already carried it. 1.30:
  स्रंसते is “slips”, not Hindi “falls”; en/ne already carry the former.
  Both exact row forms occur only in those verses across the whole book.
* 2.01: the verse addresses “him” and does not say “seeing” or name Arjuna;
  these were removed in all three literals. The contextual title still names
  Arjuna, producing a new audit advisory, 51 total / 0 blocking; its context
  remains unaltered, not mistaken for direct wording. A whole-book screen of
  contextual names and unprinted participles is still owed.
* 2.02: “closing the door of heaven” replaces the adjective अस्वर्ग्यम् with
  an image absent from the verse; all three now say not leading to heaven.
  Exact-row अस्वर्ग्यम् is unique in the 700.
* 2.42–2.43: the flowery speech is the object of the verb “speak”, not words
  in which the unwise delight; the two verses' grammar connects that speech
  to birth as the fruit of action and rites. Removed “attached to ritual and
  enjoyment” from 2.42 and corrected the linkage in all three languages. The
  unique exact rows पुष्पिताम्, वाचम्, and प्रदाम् were screened across the
  700. The interpretation of जन्मकर्मफलप्रदाम् is supported by the Śaṅkara
  commentary reported at https://theaum.org/pages/hinduism/dharma/mahabharata/bhagavad_gita/thegita/2/bg-2-43/;
  no third-party wording was transcribed.
* 2.59: निराहारस्य describes abstaining, not simply being self-controlled;
  the embodied person was omitted. All three literals restored that sense.
  The exact row निराहारस्य is unique in the 700.
* 3.04: ne/hi replaced “freedom from the bondage of action” with “actionless
  state” for नैष्कर्म्यम्; English already says freedom from action. The
  exact row नैष्कर्म्यम् is unique in the 700.
* 3.38: removed contextual parentheticals “desire” and “knowledge” from the
  literal in all three languages; they remain available in the paraphrase. A
  book-wide survey of parenthetical contextual expansions remains outstanding.
* 4.16: ne/hi rendered अशुभात् as sorrow instead of the inauspicious/evil;
  changed to अशुभ. The only other exact-row अशुभात् is 9.01, where en reads
  evil and ne/hi read पाप; the latter narrower rendering remains to evaluate.

The exact-row screens above are deliberately bounded: a compound or variant
may contain the same meaning without having the same row form. They do not
close an entire semantic error class. Full 700-verse class checks remain owed
for the contextual name/participle and parenthetical changes, and for similar
concessions and participles beyond the exact forms searched.

## Cross-check against public-domain English translations (2026-11-22)

The owner asked for the literals to be checked against a copyright-free
translation. Two were used, both public domain in the US and freely readable:
Annie Besant, *The Bhagavad Gītā or The Lord's Song* (Theosophical Publishing
Society, 1896; Internet Archive item `bhagavadgt00besagoog`, marked
NOT_IN_COPYRIGHT) and, as a second opinion where Besant is either loose or
itself interpretive, K. T. Telang's translation in *Sacred Books of the East*
vol. 8 (1882). Neither was transcribed into the data. Where Besant and the
house reading differed, the Sanskrit and the verse's own rows decided; her
wording was used as evidence that a clause was being read as stated, not as a
source to copy. Commentary was never consulted for a reading.

Both are old translations and neither is an authority: Besant paraphrases
("the flaming fire of sorrow", 2.23), keeps Sanskrit terms untranslated
(Manas, Buddhi), and in 6.07 reads परमात्मा as "the higher Self" of the man
rather than as a separate term. She was therefore used only to confirm or
contradict a *sense*, never to supply wording.

Two of these are class defects worth naming. **6.22/6.23 is a boundary

reverse direction**: here the *rows* were wrong and the literals right, which
is the case `semantic-audit.md` rule 1 warns is possible.
